# MAP — the shared universe of the seven reference papers, by concept

**Purpose.** A writer opens this before touching our paper. For each concept the field shares it answers: what it is, who has it, whether their versions are the same thing, how it got here, what the field calls and writes it *now*, how a sentence about it is worded, and whom to cite at first use. It is organised by concept, not by name, because the names are where we went wrong.

**Evidence.** The seven records in this directory — `Ber00.md`, `Pit01a.md`, `Pit01b.md`, `PS06.md`, `LM17.md`, `LM24.md`, `FLLM.md` — are the primary evidence and are cited by record and entry (e.g. "Ber00 A31", "Pit01a entry 34", "PS06 A.15", "LM17 §2.6", "LM24 A.7.2", "FLLM A.12"). Quotations below are taken from the records and are verbatim from the transcriptions the records were made from. Where a record flags a reading as unverifiable, that is carried forward. Where the records could not settle an identity, the paper itself was opened; each such check is stated where it is used. Only LM24 has a PDF in the library; every other source is a transcription (LM17 is the authors' TeX).

**Chronology and short cites.** Ber00 = Berarducci, Trans. AMS 2000. Pit01b = Pitteloud, *Existence of prime elements…*, JSL 2001. Pit01a = Pitteloud, *Algebraic properties…*, which the field cites as **APAL 116 (2002)** (PS06 B.2, LM17 §9 item 14, LM24 §B key [19]); our short cite keeps "01" for the SSRN preprint the record was made from, but a bibliography entry should say 2002. PS06 = Pommersheim–Shahriari, Proc. AMS 2006 (LM17 dates it 2005). LM17 = L'Innocente–Mantova, J. Log. Anal. 2017. LM24 = L'Innocente–Mantova, Adv. Math. 2024. FLLM = Fornasiero–Lavi–L'Innocente–Mantova, arXiv May 2024 (dated Nov 2023 in the front matter).

**Three relations used throughout.** *Identical*: same definition, possibly different dress. *Equivalent presentations*: different definitions that provably pick out the same object; the equivalence is stated and, where a paper states or proves it, cited. *Different things under a shared name or symbol*: flagged **LOUDLY** with a box like this:

> **TRAP.** …

**Fragility.** Decisions made on thin or conflicting evidence are marked **⚠ FRAGILE** inline and collected in Part IX. They are candidates for a sentence of acknowledgement in the paper.

**Editorial stance.** The default is the field's living usage — what LM24 and FLLM write, because their authors are the readers — weighed against the originating paper (almost always Ber00) and the coherence of the system a symbol belongs to. Decisions are made a *system* at a time (Part 0 lists them), so that a map, its value set and the quotient it induces come from one paper's presentation. Every form adopted already exists in some reference.

## Part I. The ambient universe

### I.1 Generalised power series and the field $K((G))$

**What it is.** A formal sum $b=\sum_{x\in G} b_x t^x$ with coefficients in a field $K$, exponents in an ordered abelian group $G$, and well-ordered support; the set of all of them is $K((G))$, a field under the convolution product.

**Who has it; identity.** All seven. The definitions are **identical** (same formal sum, same well-orderedness condition, same double-parenthesis symbol): Ber00 A1, Pit01b A.1.1/A.2.1, Pit01a entry 1, PS06 A.5–A.6, LM17 §1.3, LM24 A.0.6/A.0.8, FLLM A.1–A.2. Every paper that says where the field structure comes from says Hahn (Ber00 B1, Pit01a B1, Pit01b B.3.1, LM17 §1.3, LM24 B.4.4, FLLM B.1); PS06 says only "can be shown to be a field" (PS06 C.1). The one variant not adopted here is $K((t))^{\mathbf G}$, which Ber00 offers as an alternative and then simply does not use ("Another notation for $K((\mathbf G))$ is $K((t))^{\mathbf G}$, where the formal variable $t$ is displayed. We always use $t$ for the formal variable", §1.1 p. 553; Ber00 G2) — no paper rejects it, none uses it.

**History.** Classical (Hahn 1907); Ber00 is the paper that brought it into this problem, with the one-sentence definition every later paper paraphrases. LM24 adds the reported name "Hahn field" for $K((G))$ and the bounded version $K((G))_\kappa$ ("restricted or $\kappa$-bounded Hahn field"), needed only because $\mathbf{No}$ is a proper class (LM24 A.1.4, A.1.7). Of these, "$\kappa$-bounded" is not used again; "Hahn field" recurs once ("the canonical valuation of the Hahn field $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbf R)((\mathbf G))$", p. 60), and "restricted" is LM24's working adjective — "We call such series **restricted** or $\kappa$-**bounded**" (p. 9, before the "Hahn field" sentence) and later "restricted Dedekind-MacNeille completion" (Def 8.5.1, p. 77). LM24 also introduces $K((S))$ for an arbitrary subset $S\subseteq G$ ("the series with support contained in $S$", A.0.8, A.1.8) — this is what makes $K((G^{\le 0}))$, $K((G^{<0}))$ instances of one notation rather than ad-hoc symbols. Pitteloud had already read $K((Q^{<0}))$, $K((G_0))((H^{\le 0}))$ "by the same convention" without stating it (Pit01b A.2.4).

**Living form.** "generalised power series" (British; LM17 §0.3, LM24 A.5.4, FLLM title-note — the body of FLLM says "generalised"), and thereafter just **series**: "the series $b$", "a series of order type $\omega^2$", "series with finite support" (LM24 A.0.6: "the one-word noun is series (hundreds of occurrences)"; FLLM A.1: 47 "series" against 8 "power series"). PS06's "element of $R$" (PS06 A.5) is the outlier and should not be copied except where the ring-theoretic role is foregrounded ("irreducible elements", FLLM A.40). Never "Hahn series" (LM24 A.0.6: once, in passing) and never "transseries". Definition sentence to model at first use, LM24 p. 3: "Given a field $K$ and a totally ordered abelian group $G$, a **generalised power series** with **coefficients** in $K$ and **exponents** in $G$ is a formal sum $b=\sum_{x\in G} b_x t^x$ with $b_x\in K$ such that the support $\operatorname{supp}(b):=\{x\in G: b_x\neq 0\}$ is well-ordered, namely every non-empty subset has a minimum." Cite Hahn for the field structure if anything is cited; the object itself needs no citation.

**Phrasing.** "the coefficient of $t^x$ in $b$" (Ber00 E1, LM24 E.3: "the coefficient of $t^{\sup(p)}$ is 1", FLLM E.1); "$b$ has order type …"; "with support contained in …" (LM17 §1.2); the sequence form $b=\sum_{i<\alpha} b_i t^{x_i}$ with $(x_i)$ strictly increasing when the order type is to be displayed (LM24 A.0.7, used in LM24's statements of Ber00's and Pit01b's theorems, LM24 B.1.2, B.2.1).

### I.2 Support

**Identity.** Identical in all seven: the set of exponents with non-zero coefficient. Three symbols: $S_a$ (Ber00 A2; Pit01b A.1.2; Pit01a entry 2; LM17 §1.2), $\mathrm{Supp}(a)$ with a capital (PS06 A.4), $\operatorname{supp}(b)$ upright lower-case (LM24 A.0.6; FLLM A.1). Ber00 also writes $B$ for the support of $b$ in §7 (Ber00 A2).

**Decision (S1).** $\operatorname{supp}(b)$, upright: both 2024 papers, and it is the symbol the RV/degree apparatus is written in. Adopt with it LM24's conventions for sets of exponents: $B<C$ for "every element of $B$ is below every element of $C$", $\operatorname{supp}(c)<\operatorname{supp}(d)$, $\operatorname{supp}(b)>x$, and $B+C$ for the Minkowski sum (LM24 A.2.10; the same conventions are Ber00 A28, Pit01b A.4.1 item 5, Pit01a entries 26, 29, LM17 §1.2 — all identical, none attributes them). FLLM additionally writes $\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname{supp}(b))$ for the topological closure (FLLM A.1); Ber00 and Pit01a use an overline, $\overline B$ (Ber00 A28, Pit01a entry 39 — where a double overline is the closure in $\tilde G$). Use $\mathrm{cl}(\cdot)$ if the closure is needed often (it is the only living symbol); "the closure of the support of $b$" in words otherwise (Ber00 A38, PS06 A.16).

**Phrasing.** "$0$ is an accumulation point of $\operatorname{supp}(b)$" (LM24 A.8.3, FLLM A.27 "accumulation points"); PS06 says "limit point" (PS06 C.6), Pit01b says "cofinal to $0$" (Pit01b C.10), Ber00 says "sufficiently small final segment" (Ber00 A12). "supports are pairwise disjoint" (LM17 §5.5, FLLM A.1); "$\operatorname{supp}(b)$ intersects two distinct non-zero Archimedean classes" (LM24 E.3). The set difference is $\setminus$ (FLLM uses both $\setminus$ and $-$, FLLM A.1; PS06 uses a backslash, PS06 A.4).

### I.3 Letters: the variable, coefficients, exponents, series, ordinals

**The variable.** $t$ in Ber00 ("We always use $t$ for the formal variable", A5), PS06, LM17, LM24, FLLM. Pitteloud alone writes $x^\gamma$ (Pit01b A.0.2, Pit01a entry 1). Decision: $t$. LM24 writes surreal numbers as series in $\omega=t^{-1}$ and says so once (LM24 A.0.9: "We will consistently use series in $\omega^{-1}$ to represent surreal numbers, while keeping $t$ for series in other fields $K((G))$"); PS06 does the same substitution (PS06 A.21). Follow LM24 if $\mathbf{Oz}$ appears.

**Coefficients.** Always the series letter subscripted by the exponent: $a_\gamma$, $b_x$ (every record). FLLM writes $b_{i\gamma}$ for the coefficient of $t^\gamma$ in $b_i$ (FLLM A.1).

**Exponents.** Two dialects. Greek $\gamma,\delta,\beta,\xi$ (Ber00 A1, Pitteloud, LM17 §0.4, FLLM §1 and its proofs) and Latin $x,y,z$ (PS06 §3–4, LM24 throughout, FLLM's Definitions 2.4 and 2.1 which copy LM24). Ordinals are Greek in all papers, so the Greek-exponent dialect makes $\gamma$ do double duty (Ber00 A1 note on $\xi$; LM17 §0.4 "so $\gamma$ serves both as an exponent in $\mathbb R$ and as an ordinal"). **Decision (S1).** Since the degree apparatus (Part V) is taken from LM24 and is written with $x,y$ and ordinals $\alpha,\beta$, use $x,y,z$ for real exponents and $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ for ordinals when that apparatus is in play; if a passage is purely Berarducci-style (truncations, $J$, $v_J$), Greek exponents $\gamma,\delta$ are what FLLM itself writes there (FLLM A.15 has $b^{|\gamma}$ 188 times) and are acceptable. Do not mix within one displayed formula.

**Series.** $b,c,d$ generic (LM24 A.0.6, G.4; FLLM A.34), with $a$ the distinguished element in Ber00/Pit01b/PS06/LM17 (Pit01b A.0.9: "$a$ is reserved for the candidate prime"). LM24 reserves $p,q$ for series with finite support and $B,C,D$ for RV-elements (LM24 A.10.3, G.4); FLLM uses $p,q$ for factors and $r$ for a remainder of lower degree (FLLM A.34). Constants $k\in K$ (LM24 G.4, PS06 A.1). Adopt LM24's letters.

**Ordinals.** $\alpha,\beta$ everywhere; $\omega$, $\omega_1$ (LM24 A.2.4, FLLM A.33); $\mathbb N\ni 0$ in LM17, LM24, FLLM and Pitteloud (LM17 §0.4, LM24 A.2.4 "We identify $\mathbb N$ with $\omega$", FLLM A.6, Pit01b A.0.5 with $\mathbb N^*$ for the positive ones). Ber00 writes $k<\omega$ (Ber00 A21). Decision: $\mathbb N\ni 0$, so Conway's series is $1+\sum_{n\in\mathbb N} t^{-1/(n+1)}$ in LM24's spelling (LM24 A.0.9) — or $1+\sum_n t^{-1/n}$ with the range left implicit as LM17 §1.6 and FLLM A.6 write it. Either exists in the literature; the LM24 form is the one that does not depend on the reader's $\mathbb N$.

### I.4 The rings $K((G^{\le 0}))$, $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$, $\mathbb Z+K((G^{<0}))$; the superscript notation; standing hypotheses

**What and identity.** $K((G^{\le 0}))$ = series with non-positive exponents; identical in all seven (Ber00 A8, Pit01b A.2.3, Pit01a entry 4, PS06 A.7, LM17 §1.4, LM24 A.1.8, FLLM A.3). The superscript notation $G^{\le 0}, G^{<0}, \mathbb R^{\le 0}$ is used by all and defined by none except Pit01a ("$X^{\le\gamma}:=\{\delta\in X:\delta\le\gamma\}$", entry 28) and Pit01b (A.2.3); LM24 uses it "without comment" (A.0.8). The integer-part ring $\mathbb Z+K((G^{<0}))$ — Ber00 writes $\mathbf R((\mathbf G^{<0}))\oplus\mathbf Z$ (A8, A17), PS06 $\mathbb R((G^{<0}))\oplus\mathbb Z$ (A.7), Pit01b $\mathbb R((\mathbb R^{<0}))\oplus\mathbb Z$ (C.5), LM17 $\mathbb Z+K((G^{<0}))$ (§1.4), LM24 $\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa$ (A.0.10), FLLM $Z+K((G^{<0}))$ (A.3) — is the same object in all; the $\oplus$ of the early papers is the internal direct sum and collides with the natural sum of ordinals (Ber00 A8 note, PS06 A.21), which is presumably why the 2017–24 papers write $+$.

**Names.** No paper names $K((G^{\le 0}))$. Ber00: "the ring $K((\mathbf G^{\le 0}))$", "our ring" (A8); PS06 alone abbreviates $R=K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ and calls it "the $K$-algebra" (A.8); the others write it out every time (Pit01b A.0.3: "always the explicit symbol"; FLLM A.3: "never given a short name"). **Decision (S2).** Write $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ out. Do not introduce a letter for it.

**Bold.** LM24's bold $\mathbf K,\mathbf G,\mathbf Z,\mathbf J$ mark objects that may be proper classes; italic $K,G,H$ are sets (LM24 header, A.0.10). Ber00's bold $\mathbf G$ is a rendering the record cannot verify (Ber00 A1). Decision: plain $K$, $G$ unless proper classes are genuinely in play, in which case LM24's convention exactly.

**Standing hypotheses.** $K$ of characteristic $0$: Ber00 A52, Pit01b A.0.1, Pit01a entry 6, PS06 A.1, LM17 abstract only (§0.1), LM24 A.0.10, FLLM abstract only (A.2). Where it is used: the coefficient $k$ in the Leibniz-type formula (Ber00 A52, Pit01b A.0.1, Pit01a entry 6 — all three records infer this; no paper says it. Checked at the source: Ber00 assumes characteristic zero in §1.5 and §12 and uses $k\ne0$ silently in the proof of Lemma 8.2, where $v_J(kb^{k-1}b^{|\gamma}c^2)$ is read as $v_J(b^{k-1}b^{|\gamma}c^2)$, eq. (6) and the display after it, p. 569–570; Pit01b states it in the abstract and Cor. 2.2 comes "with no proof"; Pit01a assumes it at p. 1 ("From now on, $K$ will always denote a field of characteristic 0") and in Thm 5.1 and never invokes it, the integer coefficient $k$ entering silently in Lemma 4.6 (§7's Prop. 7.2 is the two-term identity and carries no $k$)). $G$: "ordered abelian divisible group" (Ber00 abstract, A52: six word orders, none dominant), "ordered additive abelian divisible group" (Pit01b A.0.1), "dense ordered group" — not divisible — (Pit01a entry 16, 20 occurrences), "ordered abelian group" (PS06 A.2, LM17 §1.1), "divisible ordered abelian group, possibly a proper class" (LM24 A.0.10), "ordered abelian group" (FLLM A.1). Decision: LM24's "divisible ordered abelian group" when divisibility is assumed, "totally ordered abelian group" (LM24 A.0.6) when it is not; say at the outset that $K$ has characteristic $0$, as all but LM17 and FLLM do in the body, and — unlike every reference — say where it is used if the paper uses it.

**Phrasing.** "$b$ is irreducible in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$" (the ring always named with "in": Ber00 A9, Pit01b E.2, LM24 E.2). "the subring … consisting of the generalised power series with non-positive exponents" (LM17 §1.4). Hypotheses restated in full in each numbered statement (PS06 A.1 does this; LM24 does not).

### I.5 Series with finite support

**Identity.** One object, three symbols, and the symbols collide with standard notation in two different ways. Ber00: $K[\mathbf G]$ "the ring of G-polynomials", $K[\mathbf G^{\le 0}]=K[\mathbf G]\cap K((\mathbf G^{\le 0}))$ (Ber00 A15; the name appears once). LM24: $\mathbf K(\mathbf S)$ with *single* parentheses (Notation 2.1.5, LM24 A.1.9), used dozens of times ($\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$, $\mathrm{RV}_0=\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$, $\widehat{\mathrm P}(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$, $\mathbb N(-\mathbf{On})$). FLLM: $K[\mathbb R^{\le}]$ with square brackets and — as transcribed — no "0" (FLLM A.36, G.23: unverified). PS06 and Pitteloud describe it in words. The ordinary name is "series with finite support" in all (Ber00 A15 "dominant description"; LM24 A.1.9 "dominant, dozens of times"; FLLM A.36 "the series with finite support"). LM24 never calls them polynomials in $\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ though it invokes the isomorphism with Laurent polynomial rings (A.1.9, Fact 2.1.6).

> **TRAP.** $K(G)$ in LM24 is *not* a field of rational functions, and $K[G]$ in Ber00/FLLM is the group-ring-style notation for the same finite-support ring. A reader of LM24 who sees $K[\mathbb R^{\le 0}]$ will read it correctly but will notice the dialect change; a reader who sees $K(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$ without LM24 in mind may read a function field.

**Decision (S2), ⚠ FRAGILE.** Two living papers, two symbols. Weigh: LM24 is published, uses the notation structurally (it is the residue ring $\mathrm{RV}_0$ and the coefficient ring of $\widehat{\mathrm P}(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$), and explains the choice ("Just like for $\mathbf K((\mathbf S))$", A.1.9); FLLM uses its bracket four times in passing and the rendering is unverified. Follow LM24: $K(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$, introduced in words at first use — "let $K(\mathbb R^{\le 0})$ denote the subring of $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ of the series with finite support [LM24, Notation 2.1.5]". If the paper never touches the degree/RV apparatus, the words alone suffice and no symbol need be chosen; that is the safer course. Fragile because FLLM, the latest paper and by a superset of LM24's authors, did not follow LM24 here.

**Results attached.** $K(G^{\le 0})$ and $K(G)$ are GCD domains, units of $K(G^{\le 0})$ are $K^\times$, units of $K(G)$ are the monomials (LM24 A.5.4, Fact 2.5.2, citing Gilmer–Parker); Ritt's theorem in LM24's rephrasing (LM24 A.6.1–A.6.2, Props 2.6.2–2.6.3, "unique up to reordering and up to multiplication by elements of $\mathbf K$"); irreducibles of $K[\mathbb R^{\le 0}]$ remain irreducible in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ (Ber00 A48, Thm 11.2); every series with finite support is primal (LM24 A.5.1, Cor. 6.3.9).

### I.6 Monomials, units, divisibility by a monomial

**Monomial.** Ber00: "the monomial $t^\gamma$", never defined (A10); Pit01b A.3.4, Pit01a entry 5 likewise; LM24 defines it: "Call **monomials** the series of the form $kt^x$ for $x\in G$ and $k\in K\setminus\{0\}$" (A.1.2) — note LM24's monomial carries a coefficient. LM17: "non-constant monomials" in the abstract (§2.2). Identical notion; LM24's is the only definition and it is slightly wider (coefficient allowed). Adopt LM24's sentence if a definition is wanted; in practice all papers say "$t^x$".

**Units.** "The units of $\mathbf R((\mathbf G^{\le 0}))$ are the elements of $\mathbf R$" (Ber00 A9); "The units of $K((G^{\le 0}))$, that is to say its invertible elements, are exactly the non-zero elements of $K$" (LM24 Cor. 2.1.4, A.1.13 — the only place it is stated as a result). Symbols for $K\setminus\{0\}$: $K^*$ (LM17 §0.4), $K^\times$ (FLLM A.32), "$K\setminus\{0\}$"/"non-zero elements of $K$" (LM24 A.1.2, A.1.13; FLLM A.6 "$P_0=K\setminus\{0\}$"). Decision: $K^\times$ or the words; cite [LM24, Cor. 2.1.4] when the fact is used. PS06 never states which elements are units (PS06 C.3).

**Divisible by a monomial.** The hypothesis of every irreducibility theorem: "is not divisible by any monomial $t^\gamma$ with $\gamma<0$" (Ber00 A10, Thm 10.5), "not divisible by $t^\gamma$ for any $\gamma<0$" (PS06 B.1 item 2, LM17 §2.2, FLLM B.6), "$b$ is not divisible by $t^x$ for any $x\in\mathbb R^{<0}$" (LM24 A.12.1, Theorems E, F). See IV.13 for its equivalents. The verb is "divides"/"is divisible by"; the symbol $a\mid c$ is Pitteloud's and LM17's (Pit01b A.6.1: "[$a\,|\,c$ means $a$ divides $c$]"; LM17 §2.5 "$a\vert c$ means $a$ divides $c$"), adopted by LM24 for RV too ("we say that $B$ **divides** $C$, and write $B\mid C$", A.11.3). Adopt $b\mid c$.

### I.7 The canonical valuation $v$ and the order on $K((G))$

**What.** $v(b)=\min\operatorname{supp}(b)$. Ber00: "natural valuation $v\colon K((\mathbf G))\to\mathbf G$" (A6). LM24: "The **canonical** (or $t$-**adic**) valuation" with $v(0)=-\infty$ and the inequality written $v(b+c)\ge\min\{v(b),v(c)\}$ (A.1.12 — the record notes the non-standard mix of a minimum-valuation with $-\infty$ at $0$). Pitteloud never names it; it appears only as "$\delta:=\min S_a$" in the definition of the order (Pit01a entry 3, C10). PS06: absent. LM17: absent. FLLM: absent (the bare $v$ at FLLM L411 is a slip for $\deg_J$, FLLM G.14). Identical where present.

> **TRAP.** The bare letter $v$ is the ordinal value in PS06 ("denoted by $v(a)$ (Berarducci [1] uses $v_J(a)$ and Pitteloud [6] uses $v_0(a)$)", PS06 A.15) and the min-support valuation in Ber00 and LM24. Ber00 is explicit: "($v$ is the natural valuation into $\mathbf G$, not the ordinal value)" (A6). Never write $v$ for the ordinal value.

**Decision.** "canonical valuation $v$" with LM24's sign conventions [LM24, §2.1]; Table 4.4.1 of LM24 lists $(\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le 0})),v)$ as one of the valued rings (LM24 A.10.7), so the name is load-bearing there.

**Order on $K((G))$.** Sign of the coefficient at $\min\operatorname{supp}$: Ber00 A7 ("leading coefficient", once), Pit01b A.2.2, Pit01a entry 3, LM17 §0.4 ("$0<t^\gamma<a$ for $\gamma\in G^{>0}$"). Identical; only needed if $K$ is ordered. Consequence all papers share: series with positive exponents are infinitesimal, $K((G^{\le 0}))$ has no infinitesimal part, $K((G^{<0}))$ is the infinite part (Ber00 A18, LM17 §0.4). Integer part and standard part: I.11.

### I.8 Factorisation vocabulary

**Identity.** "irreducible", "prime", "unit", "associate", "(unique) factorisation into irreducibles" are used in the standard ring-theoretic sense by all seven and *defined by none* (Ber00 A9/C7, Pit01b C.1–C.2, Pit01a C6, PS06 C.2–C.4, LM17 §3.1, LM24 C.1, FLLM C.1–C.4). The closest things to definitions: Ber00's gloss "which do not admit a factorization $a=bc$ with $b,c\notin\mathbf R$" (A9) and "non-trivial means $b,c\notin K$" (A9); Pit01b's "prime elements (i.e., elements generating prime ideals)" (C.1) and the working form "$ab=cd$ … we want to prove $a\mid c$ or $a\mid d$" (A.7.1, adopted by LM17 §3.1 "$AB=CD$. We claim that $A\vert C$ or $A\vert D$"); LM24's "$b\in R$ is prime if and only if it is irreducible and primal" (A.5.1). A non-trivial factorisation has factors in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))\setminus K$ (Ber00 A9, LM17 §4.3, Pit01a C6 "(not in $K$)").

**Spelling.** "factorization" (Ber00, PS06), "factorisation" (LM17 30×, LM24; FLLM's text has neither spelling outside its bibliography, and mixes "generalised"/"generalized"). **Decision (S9): British**, as in LM17 and LM24.

**Phrasing (S9).** "is irreducible in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$" / "is prime in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$" (Pit01b E.2 ×8, LM24 E.2); "both $b$ and $b+1$ are irreducible" (Ber00 Thm 10.5 via LM17 §2.8, FLLM B.6; LM24 writes "$1+b$", B.1.2); "admits a (unique) factorisation into irreducibles" (Pit01a entry 65 "admits at least one factorization into irreducibles", LM17 §3.1, LM24 E.2); "$K((G^{\le 0}))$ does have irreducible elements" (Ber00 E2, Pit01b E.2, Pit01a E14 — the emphatic "does have" is the field's); uniqueness: "unique up to reordering and up to multiplication by elements of $\mathbf K$" (LM24 A.6.2 "the paper's fixed uniqueness formula"), PS06 says "unique (up to units)" and "associates" (PS06 A.20, C.3), LM17 "up to a unit" (§3.1). Noun "irreducibles" is fine ("the absence of irreducibles", Ber00 A9; FLLM A.41). "factor" as a verb: PS06 "factor uniquely into irreducibles" (C.11); LM24 "$b$ factors into a product of …", "splits into irreducible factors" (E.2). "non-zero and non-unit element" (LM24 C.1 — once, p. 4; the bare compound "non-zero non-unit" does not occur in LM24) rather than Pitteloud's "(not in $K$)".

**Cite.** Nothing for the vocabulary. For the fact that $K((G^{\le 0}))$ is a non-noetherian integral domain: PS06 A.7 asserts it; LM17 §1.4 and LM24 explain it by $t^{-1}=t^{-1/2}t^{-1/2}=\dots$; the example is the field's standard one (Ber00 A10 "all the elements of $J$ are trivially reducible").

### I.9 Refinements, the refinement property, pre-Schreier, primal, GCD

**What and identity.** Three presentations of one property of a domain, plus the element-wise notion.
- *Common refinement* (Ber00 §13, reporting Conway: "whether … any two factorizations have a common refinement", B17; LM17 Def. at L184–194 defines "refinement" of a factorisation $a=b_1\cdots b_n$ formally and states Conway's conjecture as "any two factorisations of $a$ admit common refinements", §3.2).
- *Refinement property* (LM24 Conj. 1.1.1(2), A.0.5: "for all $b,c,d,e\in\mathbf{Oz}$, if $bc=de$, then there are $f,g,h,i\in\mathbf{Oz}$ such that $b=fg$, $c=hi$, $d=fh$, $e=gi$"), attributed to Conway [7, p46].
- *Pre-Schreier domain* = every element primal (LM24 A.5.2, "the original definition from [25]" Zafrullah), and "Integral domains with the refinement property are usually called pre-Schreier domains" (LM24 B.4.2).
- *Primal* element (LM24 A.5.1, from Cohn [6]): "if $b$ divides $cd$ then $b=b_1b_2$ with $b_1\mid c$, $b_2\mid d$".
These are **equivalent presentations**: the 2×2 refinement property is the primal condition for both factors at once, and common refinements of arbitrary finite factorisations follow by induction; LM24 states the equivalence of the first two with pre-Schreier (A.5.2, proof inline; B.4.2) and LM17 states Conway's conjecture in the first form without mentioning the others. No paper proves "common refinement ⟺ refinement property" explicitly, but LM24 treats Conway's question and the refinement property as one thing (A.0.5). *GCD domain*: LM24 A.5.3; every GCD domain is pre-Schreier (Fact 2.5.1); $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ is pre-Schreier iff GCD iff every irreducible with infinite support is prime (Cor. 6.4.2, A.11.9).

**History.** Conway's question (1976) → Ber00 §13 restates it as "common refinement" → LM17 formalises "refinement" and states it as Conjecture 1.4 for $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ → LM24 re-frames it as the refinement property = pre-Schreier, brings in Cohn's "primal" and Zafrullah's name, proves every finite-support series primal and $\sum\omega^{1/(n+1)}$ primal in $\mathbf{Oz}$ (A.15.4), and asks whether $t^H K((H^{\le 0}))$ is pre-Schreier (A.15.2). The keywords of FLLM still carry "pre-Schreier domain" though its body never uses it (FLLM B.23).

**Living form (S9).** LM24's: "primal in $R$" (always with "in", A.5.1), "pre-Schreier domain", "the refinement property", "a greatest common divisor" with the indefinite article and never a gcd symbol (A.5.3 — FLLM A.25 does write $\gcd(t^{\gamma_1},\dots,t^{\gamma_m})$ once), "coprime" undefined. Cite: "[LM24, §2.5]" for the definitions, Cohn [6] for primal and Zafrullah [25] for pre-Schreier as LM24 does. Use LM17's "refinement" only when talking about refining a given factorisation. Name the statement "Conway's refinement conjecture" or "Conjecture 1.1.1(2) of [LM24]" — never the bare "Conway's conjecture":

> **TRAP.** "Conway's conjecture" has **three referents** in the corpus. LM17 Conjecture 1.4 [Conway]: "For every non-zero series $a\in K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$, any two factorisations of $a$ admit common refinements" — the refinement property, and "Conway's conjecture" in LM17's Further remarks means this. LM24 §1.1 "Conway's conjectures" = Conjecture 1.1.1, which is **two** statements: (1) primality of $1+\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}\omega^{1/(n+1)}$ in $\mathbf{Oz}$ (settled, by Pitteloud and Biljakovic–Kochetov–Kuhlmann, LM24 §1.3) and (2) the refinement property (open). FLLM p. 1: "Conway [Con76], who conjectured that the series $1+\sum_n t^{-1/n}$ is irreducible in the ring of omnific integers … Conway's conjecture was proved by Berarducci [Ber00]" — a *settled irreducibility* statement. So the latest paper, by the readers this map most wants to satisfy, uses the bare phrase for a theorem of Ber00, LM17 for the open refinement property, and LM24 for both primality and refinement. A sentence "Conway's conjecture is open" is false for a reader of FLLM; "Conway's conjecture was proved by Berarducci" is false for a reader of LM17. Always qualify: "Conway's refinement conjecture [LM17, Conj. 1.4; LM24, Conj. 1.1.1(2)]", "Conway's primality conjecture [LM24, Conj. 1.1.1(1)]", "Conway's irreducibility question, answered in [Ber00, Thm. 12.1]".

### I.10 Surreal numbers, omnific integers, Conway's series, Gonshor's series, Conway's and Gonshor's questions

**Identity.** Same objects throughout; the *presentation* changes. Ber00: "the huge ring of omnific integers, which is an integer part of the real closed field of surreal numbers" (A55, B6), Conway's group a proper class. Pit01b: 'the huge "ring" of omnific integers' with scare quotes (F.5). FLLM: "$\mathbb Z+\mathbb R((G^{<0}))$ (modulo some set-theoretic details which are irrelevant here)" (A.3).

**Conway's series.** $\sum_n t^{-1/n}+1$ (Ber00 A50, with the sign change from Conway explained, G1; PS06 B.3), $x^{-1}+x^{-1/2}+x^{-1/3}+\dots+1$ (Pit01b A.8.7), $1+\sum_n t^{-1/n}$ (LM17 §1.6, FLLM B.3), $1+\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}t^{-1/(n+1)}$ / $1+\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}\omega^{1/(n+1)}$ (LM24 A.0.9). On the other hand, the primality of $\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega+1$ was not addressed." LM24's own re-derivation is Examples 9.2.8 ("recall that this could also be deduced from [4,18]"). Cite it as [Pit01b, Thm. 3.3] with [BKK06, Thm. 4.12] (and "[LM24, Examples 9.2.8]" for the 2024 re-proof), never as "[LM24, Thm. B]". **LM24 Theorem B** is exactly "*The omnific integer $\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega+1$ is prime in $\mathbf{Oz}$*" (p. 5; proof p. 66) — Gonshor's series only. The refinement property, Conj. 1.1.1(2), is open (I.9). Gonshor's: primality of $\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega+1$ (answered LM24 Thm B). FLLM B.7 credits Pitteloud's theorem with "answering a question of Gonshor" — a different question (existence of primes), so **"Gonshor's question" is two questions** in the records: existence of irreducibles/primes (FLLM's framing; Pit01b itself never names Gonshor alone — it writes "Conway and Gonshor (see [2, 4]) considered the problem of existence of non-standard irreducible (respectively prime) elements" in the abstract and "answering a question of Conway and Gonshor" on p. 1207) and primality of $\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega+1$ (LM24). ### I.11 Integer parts, truncation closure, standard parts, open induction

**Identity.** Integer part: Ber00 A17 ("an ordered subring $Z$ having 1 as its least positive element and such that for each $a\in F$ there is $b\in Z$ (necessarily unique) such that $b\le a<b+1$"), LM17 §1.4 (Mourgues–Ressayre cited; note LM17 uses "integer part" for both the subring and the element $\lfloor x\rfloor$), LM24 A.0.4 (Remark 2.1.2, with $\lfloor k\rfloor$), FLLM C.5 (undefined), Pit01a C7 ("models of weak axioms for arithmetic") / Pit01b C.5 ("model of open induction"). ## Part II. Ordinals

### II.1 The class of all ordinals

**Identity.** One object, two symbols. $OR$ in Ber00 ("We denote by $OR$ the class of all ordinals", A21), Pit01b (A.4.1), Pit01a (entry 7), PS06 (A.22); bold $\mathbf{On}$ in LM17 (§0.2, macro `\on`; used without introduction, §6), LM24 (A.2.3, "The (proper) class of all ordinals is denoted by $\mathbf{On}$"), FLLM (A.9, "the class $\mathbf{On}$ of ordinal numbers"). Limit ordinals: $LIM$ (Ber00 A21), $\mathrm{Lim}$ (Pit01b A.4.1 — and the recurring "$\mathrm{Lim}\cup\{0\}$" for writing every $v_0$-value as $\omega^{\delta+n}$); the later papers use the word. **Decision (S3): $\mathbf{On}$**, the word "limit". Cite nothing. LM24 fixes von Neumann ordinals and NBG as the set-theoretic frame (A.2.1–A.2.2) — only needed if proper classes are used; LM17 takes ordinals as order types (§6, "Convention: ordinals are order types (equivalence classes), not von Neumann sets"). These are different foundations for the same arithmetic and no paper's results depend on the choice; say nothing unless classes matter, then follow LM24.

### II.2 Classical arithmetic, Cantor normal form, and LM24's hats

**Classical operations.** Defined by recursion on the second argument in Ber00 (A21), Pit01a (entry 8), LM17 (§6), LM24 (A.2.5); identical. Symbols $+,\cdot$ or juxtaposition in all but LM24, which hats them: $\alpha\mathbin{\hat+}\beta$, $\alpha\mathbin{\hat\cdot}\beta$, "to prevent confusion with the field operations on the surreal numbers" (LM24 A.2.5, F.2); exponentiation is *not* hatted. FLLM, a year later, writes unhatted $+$ (FLLM A.9: "$3\omega+1$", "$\omega+\omega$"). **Decision (S3): unhatted $+,\cdot$ unless surreal numbers appear in the same text**, in which case LM24's hats exactly as LM24 uses them (order types $\omega\mathbin{\hat+}1$, $\omega\mathbin{\hat\cdot}\ell\mathbin{\hat+}m$). LM24 itself slips into unhatted sums in places (A.2.5, G.2), which shows the cost of the convention.

**Cantor normal form.** Written with **repeated terms and no finite coefficients** — $\alpha=\omega^{\beta_1}+\dots+\omega^{\beta_n}$, $\beta_1\ge\dots\ge\beta_n$ — in Ber00 (A23), Pit01b (A.4.4), PS06 (A.22), LM17 (§6), LM24 (A.2.7), FLLM (A.10). Pit01a alone writes it with coefficients, $\omega^{\alpha_1}n_1+\dots+\omega^{\alpha_k}n_k$, $\alpha_1>\dots>\alpha_k$, $n_i\in\mathbb N^*$ (entry 11), and his natural operations are defined on that form (entries 13–14). Equivalent presentations, obviously; but the choice changes what "the terms of the Cantor normal form" means, and the 2024 papers count terms ("$n$ is at most the number of terms in the Cantor Normal Form of $\deg(b)$", LM24 A.11.6; "the Cantor normal form has length three", FLLM A.10). **Decision (S3): repeated terms**, and speak of "the first term", "the last term", "the last infinite term" (LM17 §2.6, §6), "the number of terms". Where a finite multiple is needed write $\omega^\alpha\cdot m$ (PS06 A.15 "$\omega^2\cdot 2+\omega\cdot 5+1$") or LM24's $\omega\mathbin{\hat\cdot}\ell$; FLLM's "$3\omega$", "$m\omega^\alpha$" with the integer on the left is unverified and non-standard (FLLM G.15) — do not copy it. Capitalisation: "Cantor normal form" (Ber00, LM17 §0.3 12×, FLLM 7×; LM24 varies, A.2.7; FLLM varies, A.10). Pit01a/Pit01b abbreviate "C.n.f." and decorate equalities $\overset{C.n.f.}{=}$ (Pit01a entry 11) — not adopted by anyone later.

### II.3 Hessenberg's natural sum and product $\oplus$, $\odot$

**Identity.** Identical operations and identical symbols in all seven (Ber00 A24, Pit01b A.4.3, Pit01a entries 13–14, PS06 A.23, LM17 §6, LM24 A.2.8, FLLM A.9). Definitions via Cantor normal form (merge the exponents; $\omega^\alpha\odot\omega^\beta=\omega^{\alpha\oplus\beta}$ and distribute). Ber00 also proves strict monotonicity itself (Lemma 3.5, A24), which PS06 cites as "(Lemma 3.5 of Berarducci [1])" (PS06 A.23) and LM24 states as Fact 2.2.1 "ordered semiring" (A.2.8, Pit01a entry 14 "ordered semi-ring"). LM24 and FLLM add the isomorphism $(\mathbf{On},\oplus,\odot)\cong(\mathbb N[X_0,X_1,\dots],+,\cdot)$, $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}\mapsto X_\alpha$ (LM24 A.2.8, FLLM A.9) — the cleanest way to say what $\odot$ is, and the reason "multiplicatively principal" ordinals are the variables.

**Names and attribution.** "natural sum"/"natural product" (Ber00 A24, citing Hausdorff and Pohlers, with "Hessemberg's product" [sic] as an alias once, B11; Pit01b B.4.1 citing Pohlers; Pit01a entry 14 — "natural product" only, $\oplus$ and $\odot$ introduced as "commutative variants of $+$ and $\cdot$" (p. 4), with Pohlers cited for §1 as a whole ("All results of this § are well-known; you can find them for instance in Pohlers [Po]") rather than at the definitions, and "natural sum" never written; PS06 A.23 "never Hessenberg"), "Hessenberg's natural sum and product" (LM17 §6 no reference; LM24 A.2.8 citing Hessenberg [12]; FLLM A.9 "Hessenberg's natural (commutative) operations"). **Decision (S3): "Hessenberg's natural sum and product"**, symbols $\oplus,\odot$, cite Hessenberg [12] as LM24 does. ⚠ Do not copy LM24's *displayed* definition of $\odot$ verbatim: the published text (p. 13, checked in `lm.pdf` and in the transcription) reads $\alpha\odot\beta:=\bigoplus_{1\le i\le n}\bigoplus_{n+1\le j\le n+m}\omega^{\gamma_j\oplus\gamma_j}$ — a typo for $\omega^{\gamma_i\oplus\gamma_j}$; LM17 §2.1 has the same slip plus a stray "$k+m$" in the index range. FLLM p. 3 has the indices right ($\bigoplus_{1\le i\le n,\,n+1\le j\le n+m}\omega^{\gamma_i\oplus\gamma_j}$). Write the correct form, or define $\odot$ by $\omega^\alpha\odot\omega^\beta=\omega^{\alpha\oplus\beta}$ and distributivity. LM24 reuses $\oplus$ for the sum in the Dedekind–MacNeille completion (A.14.1, with a remark); Ber00/PS06/Pit01b reuse it for internal direct sums (I.4). Do not reuse it. Note LM24 extends $\oplus$ to $\mathbf{On}_\infty$ by $(-\infty)\oplus\alpha=-\infty$ (A.2.8), needed for the degree.

**Phrasing.** "the natural product is strictly increasing" (PS06 E.4), "Since $v_J(B)\odot v_J(C)=v_J(A)$, we must have that either $v_J(B)=\omega$ or …" (LM17 §3.7), "there are no $\beta,\gamma\neq 0$ such that $\beta\oplus\gamma=\omega^\alpha$" (FLLM A.9). Pitteloud's idiom of writing "$=\dots\odot\dots=\dots\,\dots$" to flag that natural and ordinal product coincide in a case (Pit01a entry 14) is his alone; Ber00's Remark 6.5 states when they coincide ("if $\rho\in\mathbf{MP}$, $\alpha\in\mathbf H$ and $\rho\ge\alpha$, then $\rho\alpha=\rho\odot\alpha$", A39).

### II.4 Additively / multiplicatively principal ordinals; $\mathbf H$, $\mathbf{MP}$; the edge at $0$

**Identity — with an edge.** "Additive(ly) principal" = cannot be written as an ordinal sum of two strictly smaller ordinals = of the form $\omega^\alpha$: Ber00 Def 3.1 (A22, *$0$ is additive principal*, Fact 3.2), PS06 A.22 ($\mathbf H$ includes $0$), LM24 Def 3.3.1 (A.9.2: "$\alpha=\omega^\beta$ for some $\beta$; equivalently … and $\alpha\neq 0$" — *$0$ excluded*), FLLM A.11 (two clauses that disagree at $0$, as Ber00's do not). Pitteloud never uses the term ("just 'the ordinals $\omega^\alpha$'", Pit01a entry 10). So the versions are identical except at $0$, where Ber00/PS06 include it (so that $v_J$ takes values in $\mathbf H$) and LM24 excludes it (so that "weakly principal" means non-zero). "Multiplicative(ly) principal" = of the form $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$: Ber00 Def 3.6/Fact 3.8 (A26; the record notes Def 3.6 and Fact 3.8 disagree at $\alpha=2$), LM24 p. 54 ("multiplicatively principal, i.e. of the form $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$", A.9.2), FLLM p. 3 ("cannot be written as a product of two strictly smaller ordinals (equivalently, it is of the form $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$)", A.11 — two clauses that disagree on **infinitely many ordinals**, not just at the bottom: "cannot be written as a product of two strictly smaller ordinals" is satisfied by $0$, $1$, $2$, by every prime number ($3\ne\beta\gamma$ with $\beta,\gamma<3$), by $\omega+1$, and by many more, none of the form $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$. The clause is the wrong generalisation of "additively principal": the correct one is Ber00's Def 3.6, "$\alpha>0$ and for every $\beta,\gamma<\alpha$ we have $\beta\gamma<\alpha$" (closure of the initial segment under products, not indecomposability), which fails at $3$ ($2\cdot2=4\ge3$) as it should. Ber00's own edge is small: Def 3.6 admits $1$ and $2$ ($0\cdot0$, $0\cdot1$, $1\cdot1<2$) while Fact 3.8 admits $1$ and the $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$ only, so Ber00's disagreement is at $2$ alone. FLLM's *additive* clause, by contrast, disagrees with "$\omega^\alpha$" only at $0$, as Ber00's does not). The working definition everywhere is "of the form $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$", and nothing in any paper depends on the defective clause.

**Symbols.** $\mathbf H$ and $\mathbf{MP}$ are Ber00's (A22, A26; $\mathbf H$ presumably for *Hauptzahl*, B12 — an inference: Ber00 gives no reason for the letter and the word does not occur in the paper); PS06 keeps $\mathbf H$ (A.22); nobody after uses either. Grammar: Ber00 "additive principal", PS06 same, LM24/FLLM "additively principal".

**Decision (S3).** "additively principal", "multiplicatively principal" in words, defined as "of the form $\omega^\beta$" / "$\omega^{\omega^\beta}$" (LM24 Def 3.3.1 sentence), with $0$ excluded as in LM24 (and say so if $0$ matters); no $\mathbf H$, $\mathbf{MP}$ unless a class symbol is unavoidable, in which case Ber00's with a citation. Phrasing: "$\deg(b)$ is additively principal" (LM24 A.12.1, Cor. 7.1.2), "ordinals of non-additively principal Cantor degree" (FLLM abstract, A.10).

### II.5 Principal part (last term) versus Cantor degree (first exponent) — two different things

> **TRAP.** Ber00's **principal part** of $\alpha=\omega^{\alpha_1}+\dots+\omega^{\alpha_n}$ is the *last, smallest* term $\omega^{\alpha_n}$ (Fact 3.3, A23, and the paragraph after it, p. 561: "every sufficiently small final segment of a non-zero ordinal $\alpha$ has order type equal to the principal part of $\alpha$"). PS06 copies this (A.22, "principal part is the last (smallest) term, not the leading term"). LM24's **degree** of $\alpha$ and FLLM's **Cantor degree** $\deg(\alpha)$ is the *first, largest* exponent $\beta_1$ (LM24 A.2.9 "the exponent $\beta_1$ in its Cantor Normal form when $\alpha>0$, or $-\infty$ if $\alpha=0$"; FLLM A.10 "We let $\beta_1$ be the Cantor degree of $\alpha$"). The words "principal" and "degree" both suggest "leading"; only one of them is.

**Relation to the series invariants.** $v_J(b)$ is the *principal part* of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ (when $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ is a limit and $\sup\operatorname{supp}(b)=0$, Ber00 A32; PS06 A.15 spells out both the limit and the successor case; LM17 Rem. 2.5 "the last infinite term"; Pit01b A.5.1 Remark 3 "$v_0(b)=\omega^{\alpha_n}$"). $\omega^{\deg(b)}$ is the *first* term of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ (LM24 A.2.9 "$\deg(b)=\deg(\operatorname{ot}(b))$"; LM17 Rem. 4.3 for the head order-value, IV.11). So the two ends of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ are the two valuations of Parts IV and V.

**Decision (S3).** Use $\deg(\alpha)$, "Cantor degree", for the first exponent (FLLM's name; LM24's symbol and definition — they agree), and **avoid "principal part"** altogether: say "the last term of the Cantor normal form" (LM17 §2.6) when Berarducci's notion is meant. The word "principal" is already carrying "additively principal", "principal series" and "principal value" (FLLM A.11 counts three uses; with Ber00's "principal part" it would be four).

### II.6 Order type of a set and of a series; countability over $\mathbb R$

**Identity.** $\operatorname{ot}(b):=\operatorname{ot}(\operatorname{supp}(b))$ in all seven (Ber00 A29 Def 5.2, Pit01b A.1.5, Pit01a entry 9, PS06 A.9, LM17 §2.1, LM24 A.0.7, FLLM A.4). Symbol $ot$ italic or unknown in the transcribed papers, upright $\operatorname{ot}$ in LM17 (macro), LM24, FLLM. Pit01a also "o.t" twice (entry 9, unverified). **Decision: upright $\operatorname{ot}$**, applied to both sets and series (LM24 A.2.2 defines it for sets first). Order type of a set: undefined in Ber00 (C1), defined via von Neumann ordinals in LM24 (A.2.2) and as an equivalence class in LM17 (§6).

**Countability.** "the order types of the well-ordered subsets of $\mathbf G$ are exactly the countable ordinals" for $\mathbf G=\mathbf R$ (Ber00 A29), PS06 A.9, LM17 §2.1 "hence $\operatorname{ot}(a)$ is a countable ordinal", LM24 Remark 3.1.5 (A.8.5: "the set of countable ordinals is denoted by $\omega_1$"), FLLM A.33 ("all well ordered subsets of $\mathbb R$ are countable, thus $\operatorname{ot}$, $v_J$, $\deg_J$ and $\deg$ all take values below $\omega_1$"). Identical; unproved everywhere. Living phrasing: LM24's and FLLM's, with $\alpha\in\omega_1$ as the standing range of degrees (LM24 A.8.5 "$\alpha\in\omega_1$ is the standing range of degrees in §5–§7"; FLLM A.29 "for $\alpha<\omega_1$").

**Phrasing.** "has order type $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$" (everyone), "of order type $\omega$ or $\omega+1$" (Pit01b A.1.5, LM17 §2.1), "a series of order type $\omega\mathbin{\hat+}2$" (LM24), "of order types $\omega^2$ and $\omega^3$" (FLLM A.4). Pit01b's coinages "$\omega$-series", "primes of type $\omega+1$" (A.1.5) were not taken up.

### II.7 The order-type inequalities for sums, unions, products

**Identity.** $\operatorname{ot}(B\cup C)\le\operatorname{ot}(B)\oplus\operatorname{ot}(C)$ and $\operatorname{ot}(B+C)\le\operatorname{ot}(B)\odot\operatorname{ot}(C)$ — Ber00 Lemmas 4.1, 4.5 (A30), hence $\operatorname{ot}(b+c)\le\operatorname{ot}(b)\oplus\operatorname{ot}(c)$, $\operatorname{ot}(bc)\le\operatorname{ot}(b)\odot\operatorname{ot}(c)$ — Ber00 Remark 5.4 (A33). Reproduced without attribution in LM17 ("One can verify that", §2.1), with a bare attribution in Pit01a — "In order to prove $(B)$, he first shows that (see [B]): **Lemma 3.2**" (p. 10), whose clause b) is the product inequality $\operatorname{ot}(bc)\le\operatorname{ot}(b)\odot\operatorname{ot}(c)$ for *series*, with the set version $\operatorname{ot}(E+F)\le\operatorname{ot}(E)\odot\operatorname{ot}(F)$ as **Lemma 3.2 bis** ("The proof of Lemma 3.2 also works for sets"); the sum inequality for order types is not stated there (Lemma 3.2 a) is the ultrametric inequality for $v_0$) — and with pinpoint attribution in LM24 (Fact 2.2.3 "([2, Lem. 4.1])", "([2, Lem. 4.5])"; Prop. 3.1.1 "([2, Remark 5.4])", B.1.5). Identical. Ber00's Lemma 4.7 (unions of a $\lambda$-indexed family, A30) is cited by FLLM B.21 ("It follows by [Ber00, Lem. 4.7] that"). Pit01a's Lemma 3.3 (entry 42: "if $v_\gamma(d)\ge\omega^\theta$ for all $\gamma\in X$ and $v_0(X)=\omega^\beta$, then $v_0(d)\ge\omega^{\theta+\beta}$", with $X\subseteq\tilde G^{<0}$) is the $\tilde G$ analogue not of Lemma 4.7 but of Ber00's **Lemma 6.9** (IV.6), the two-series lifting lemma that Lemma 4.7 proves.

**Living form.** State them as LM24 does and cite "[Ber00, Lem. 4.1, Lem. 4.5]" for sets, "[Ber00, Rem. 5.4]" for series. The accompanying remark that the inequalities "may well be strict" (LM17 §2.1) and Ber00's "there is no way of predicting the order type of a product $bc$ given the order types of $b$ and $c$ (e.g. take $c=b^{-1}$)" (E4) are the standard motivation for passing to $v_J$ or to $\deg$.

**Equality.** $\operatorname{ot}(bc)=\operatorname{ot}(b)\odot\operatorname{ot}(c)$ when both order types are of the form $\omega^\beta$ — Ber00 Cor. 9.9 (A36), restated as "Fact 3.4.1 ([2, Corollary 9.9]). If $b,c$ are weakly principal then $\operatorname{ot}(bc)=\operatorname{ot}(b)\odot\operatorname{ot}(c)$" in LM24 (A.9.5, "Berarducci's Fact 3.4.1") and as "$P_\alpha\cdot P_\beta\subseteq P_{\alpha\oplus\beta}$ ([Ber00, Cor. 9.9])" in FLLM (B.8). FLLM adds that Ber00's remark about "few cancellations" in the product is false and not needed (FLLM F.1, Example 2.3) — the only correction of Ber00 in the corpus; Ber00 A36 has the quoted phrase. A writer who repeats Berarducci's heuristic should know this.

## Part III. The ideal $J$ and working modulo it

### III.1 The ideal $J$ — three equivalent presentations

**What.** The ideal of $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ (or $K((G^{\le 0}))$) of series "trivially reducible" because a negative monomial divides them.

**Three presentations, all in Ber00.** (a) *Generated by the negative monomials*: "the ideal generated by all the monomials $t^\gamma$ with $\gamma<0$" (Ber00 Def 5.1, A10; Pit01b A.3.3 Remark; Pit01a entry 35; LM17 Def 2.1; LM24 A.7.1 "generated by the series of the form $t^x$ for $x<0$"; FLLM A.5 §2). (b) *Divisible by a monomial*: "An element is in $J$ iff it is divisible by a monomial" (Ber00 A10; PS06 A.10 "the ideal of all elements that are divisible by $t^\gamma$ for some $\gamma<0$"; LM17 intro; FLLM A.5 §1). (c) *Support bounded away from $0$*: "$b\in J$ iff $\exists\gamma\in\mathbf G$ such that $S_b\le\gamma<0$" (Ber00 A10; Pit01b A.3.3 takes this as the definition; Pit01a entry 35 likewise; LM17 Rem. 2.2). Ber00 asserts all three equivalences in two sentences ("Clearly $J$ consists of all the series with negative support bounded away from zero", A10). Equivalent presentations of one ideal; **identical** across the seven.

**Symbol.** $J$ everywhere; bold $\mathbf J$ in LM24 because $\mathbf K$ may be a class (A.7.1; note LM24 keeps the *function* subscript italic, $v_J$, G.1). Pit01a's $J'=J\cap Re$ is local (entry 35). Ber00's $\mathcal M$ (all series without constant term) appears once to say $J$ is not maximal (A11).

**Decision (S4).** $J$, "the ideal $J$ generated by the monomials $t^x$ for $x\in\mathbb R^{<0}$", cite [Ber00, Def. 5.1] at first use; give presentation (c) as a remark if truncations are used ("$b\in J$ if and only if there exists $\gamma<0$ such that $\operatorname{supp}(b)\le\gamma$", LM17 Rem. 2.2 / Ber00). Phrasing: "an element not in $J$" (PS06), "$b\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))\setminus J$ (equivalently, … not divisible by $t^\gamma$ for any $\gamma<0$)" (LM17 §2.2, FLLM B.6 — the living gloss of Berarducci's hypothesis), "Note that $a\in J$ if and only if $v_J(a)=0$" (LM17 §2.2; Ber00 E6 "$J$ is the set of all $x$ with $v_J(x)=0$").

### III.2 Congruence modulo $J$ (and modulo a subspace)

**Four dialects for one relation.** (i) $\equiv\ \bmod J$: Ber00 ("In the sequel we write $\equiv$ for congruence modulo the ideal $J$", A13; also "$c\equiv b\bmod J+K$"), LM17 (Notation 2.3: "Let $V\subseteq K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ be any $K$-vector space. Then, let us write, for $a,b\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$, $a\equiv b\mod V$ if $a-b\in V$", §2.4 — the only *general* definition, covering $J$, $J+K$, $J_{\omega^\alpha}$), LM24 ("$c\equiv b\mod\mathbf J+\mathbf K$", "$c\equiv bd\mod\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$", A.7.2, A.12.2), FLLM (in Def. of $v_J$ and Prop. 2.9 only, A.35). (ii) "$a=b\bmod(J)$" with an equals sign and the modulus in parentheses: Pit01b (A.6.1; ×96 in the transcription — 86 `\bmod` and 10 `mod\,` — against 0 occurrences of $\equiv$; "Never $\equiv$"), Pit01a (entry 36; "The sign $\equiv$ is never used"). LM17 and FLLM slip into "$=\ \bmod$" where quoting Pitteloud (LM17 §9 item 4; FLLM A.35 "The '$=$ … mod' form dominates in §4"). (iii) Bars and tildes: PS06's $\overline a\in\overline R=R/J$ and $\widetilde a\in\widetilde R=R/(J+K)$ (A.11–A.12), with "$\overline{(bc)^{|\gamma}}=\sum\overline{b^{|\beta}}\ \overline{c^{|\xi}}$" for the convolution formula (B.1 item 5). (iv) Cosets: LM17's "$a+J$" for the germ, "$a^{|\delta}+J+K$" (§2.3, §5.3).

All four express the same relation; (iii) is PS06's alone and is the least transparent for a reader of the 2024 papers.

**Decision (S4).** LM17's Notation 2.3, $a\equiv b\mod V$, with $V$ any of $J$, $J+K$, $J_{\omega^\alpha}$, written after the whole equation ("$(ab)^{|\gamma}\equiv\sum_{\delta+\varepsilon=\gamma}a^{|\delta}b^{|\varepsilon}\mod J$", LM17 Prop. 2.9). It is Berarducci's sign, LM24's sign, and the only dialect with a stated general definition. Write "modulo $J+K$" in words when the modulus is a noun (LM17 §2.4). Do not write Pitteloud's "$=\ \bmod(J)$" even when quoting his Proposition 3.2 — LM24 Prop. 7.2.6 translates it into $\equiv$ (LM24 B.2.2), and that translation is the model.

### III.3 $J+K$; the quotient $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))/J$; the word "germ"

**$J+K$.** "Let $J+K$ be the additive subgroup of $K((\mathbf G^{\le 0}))$ generated by the ideal $J$ and the additive subgroup $K$" (Ber00 Def 5.2, A14); PS06 A.12 ("$K$ is a subspace of $R$ and thus so is $J+K$"); LM17 §2.2 (19× with spaces; "$J_{\omega^1}=J+K$"); LM24 A.12.2 ("$\mathbf J_{\omega^1}=\mathbf J_\omega=\mathbf J+\mathbf K$"); FLLM A.5 — in FLLM's indexing $J_1=J+K$, but this is the map's inference from Def. 2.11 ($J_\alpha:=\{b:\deg_J(b)<\alpha\}$): FLLM never writes $J_1$ or $J_0$. Identical. It is a $K$-vector space, not an ideal; LM17 stresses "is just a $K$-vector space" (§3.6), PS06 warns that the tilde is not multiplicative ("$\widetilde k\widetilde a=0\neq\widetilde{ka}$", A.12). Membership: "$b\in(J+K)\setminus J$" (LM24, FLLM), "$a\notin J+K$" (PS06 ×5), "$\widetilde a\neq 0$" (PS06 ×10, equivalent by PS06 A.12). See IV.13.

**The quotient.** $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))/J$ written out (Ber00 A12 "never given a letter"; LM17 "the quotient ring of germs $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))/J$", §2.3; LM24 "$K((G^{\le 0}))_{/J}$" with subscript slash, A.4.1), $\overline R$ (PS06 A.11), never named in FLLM (A.5: "instead the paper writes $\equiv\dots\mod J$"). $v_J$ passes to the quotient with the same symbol (Ber00 A31; Pit01a entry 34; LM17 §2.6 introduces $\overline v_J$ "and then immediately drops it"). Decision: write the quotient out; reuse $v_J$ on it with LM17's sentence "With a slight abuse of notation, we will use the symbol $v_J$ for both".

**"Germ" — identical object, shifting word.** Ber00 Def 5.1: elements of $K((\mathbf G^{\le 0}))/J$ are "germs of power series"; the informal gloss "Two series (both not in $J$) have the same germ if they have a common final part" is §1.6 (p. 558; A12), and Def 5.1's own version is "Two series $b,c\in K((\mathbf G^{\le0}))$ have the same germ iff for every $\gamma\le0$ sufficiently close to zero, the coefficients of $t^\gamma$ in $b$ and $c$ coincide" (p. 563); "the germ of $b$ at $\gamma$" is the class of $b^{|\gamma}$ mod $J$ (Def 6.1, A37). PS06: "germ of $a$ at zero" $=\overline a$, "germ of $b$ at $\gamma$" $=\overline{b^{|\gamma}}$ — identical to Ber00's — **plus** "germ of $a$ to the left of zero" $=\widetilde a$ and "to the left of $\gamma$" $=\widetilde{b^{|\gamma}}$, the classes modulo $J+K$ (A.11, A.12, A.14) — PS06's own extension, unique to PS06. LM17: "we call the germ of $a$ the coset $a+J$" (§2.3, 70 occurrences including "germ-like"), "germ of $a$ at $\gamma$" (§2.9) — identical to Ber00, unattributed (§2.3 note). LM24: the word does not occur (C.10: nothing imported from [15]). FLLM: defined once in Def. 2.4 and never used again (A.15 "The paper then avoids 'germ' and writes '… mod $J$' instead").

**Decision.** "germ" is Berarducci's word and LM17's; it is absent from the two latest papers, which write cosets and congruences. Use it sparingly and only in Berarducci's two senses (element of the quotient; class of $b^{|\gamma}$ mod $J$), citing [Ber00, Def. 5.1, Def. 6.1]; never PS06's "to the left of" (say "modulo $J+K$"). When a statement is about classes, the 2024 style is "$\mathrm{rv}_J(b)$" (V.5) or "$b\equiv c\mod J$", not "the germ of $b$".

### III.4 The spaces $J_{\omega^\alpha}$ — and the index trap with FLLM's $J_\alpha$

**What.** The $K$-vector space of series of ordinal value below a given bound.

**History.** Pit01b p. 1209 defines only the *relations* "$a=b\bmod(J_{\omega^\alpha})$ iff $v_0(b-a)<\omega^\alpha$" and "$a\mid b\bmod(J_{\omega^\alpha})$" — "$J_{\omega^\alpha}$ is never defined as a set" (Pit01b A.6.1); the subscript is always a $v_0$-value, usually written $J_{v_0(\cdot)}$ (×66). LM17 Def 3.1, "[Pitteloud2001, p. 1209]", makes it a set: "$J_{\omega^\alpha}:=\{a\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0})):v_J(a)<\omega^\alpha\}$", notes "$J_{\omega^0}=J$ and $J_{\omega^1}=J+K$", and that it "is closed under multiplication iff $\alpha=\omega^\beta$ for some $\beta$, and it is an ideal iff $\alpha=0$" (§3.6). LM24 Def 7.2.2 "([18, p. 1209])" copies LM17's set with bold $\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$ and the same examples (A.12.2); Remark 7.2.4 identifies $\mathbf J_{\omega^{\alpha+1}}/\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$ with $\mathrm P_\alpha$ (A.11.1). FLLM Def 2.11: "$J_\alpha:=\{b:\deg_J(b)<\alpha\}$" (A.21), "$J_\alpha$ is a $K$-vector space, and for $\alpha$ additively principal also a ring"; "$\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha=J_{\alpha+1}/J_\alpha$" (A.23).

> **TRAP.** Pitteloud/LM17/LM24 index by the **$v_J$-bound** $\omega^\alpha$; FLLM indexes by the **$\deg_J$-bound** $\alpha$. The *sets* coincide — $\{v_J<\omega^\alpha\}=\{\deg_J<\alpha\}$ — so FLLM's $J_\alpha$ is Pitteloud's $J_{\omega^\alpha}$, but the *symbols* do not: Pitteloud's $J_\omega$ is $J+K$ (LM17 §2.2, LM24 A.12.2: "$\mathbf J_{\omega^1}=\mathbf J_\omega=\mathbf J+\mathbf K$"), while FLLM's $J_\omega=\{\deg_J<\omega\}=$ Pitteloud's $J_{\omega^\omega}$. In FLLM's indexing $J_1=J+K$ is Pitteloud's $J_{\omega}$ and $J_0=J$ is Pitteloud's $J_1=J_{\omega^0}$ (these two instances are the map's, unpacked from Def. 2.11; FLLM itself never spells out $J_0$ or $J_1$). LM24 does write the bare indices, as $v_J$-bounds: "For clarity, note for instance that $\mathbf J_{\omega^0}=\mathbf J_1=\mathbf J$, $\mathbf J_{\omega^1}=\mathbf J_\omega=\mathbf J+\mathbf K$" (Def 7.2.2, p. 57). So a bare "$J_1$" is $J$ in LM24 and $J+K$ in FLLM. A reader who knows LM24 and sees $J_\alpha$ with a bare ordinal will mis-scale by an exponential unless told.

**Decision (S4), ⚠ FRAGILE.** Index by the $v_J$-bound: $J_{\omega^\alpha}$, citing "[Pit01b, p. 1209]" and "[LM17, Def. 3.1]" / "[LM24, Def. 7.2.2]" for the set form. Reason: three papers against one, the originating paper among them, and it keeps $J_{\omega^\alpha}$ legible next to $v_J$ and next to Pitteloud's Proposition 3.2 as LM24 restates it ("$a^kb\equiv c^ld\mod\mathbf J_{v_J(a^kb)}$", LM24 B.2.2). Fragile because FLLM is the latest paper and its indexing is the natural one if the paper's primary measure is $\deg_J$ (IV.4): a paper written entirely in $\deg_J$ should consider FLLM's $J_\alpha$ and then must say, once, "$J_\alpha=\{b:\deg_J(b)<\alpha\}$, so that $J_0=J$, $J_1=J+K$; this is $J_{\omega^\alpha}$ in the notation of [Pit01b, LM17, LM24]". Whichever is chosen, the sentence identifying the two must appear.

### III.5 Divisibility modulo $J_{\omega^\alpha}$

**Identity.** "$a\mid b\bmod(J_{\omega^\alpha})$ iff $\exists e,\varepsilon$ such that $b=ae+\varepsilon$ and $v_0(\varepsilon)<\omega^\alpha$" (Pit01b A.6.1); "we write $b\vert a\mod J_{\omega^\alpha}$ if there exists $c$ such that $a\equiv bc\mod J_{\omega^\alpha}$" (LM17 Def 3.1, §2.5); LM24 Def 7.2.2 identical with $\mid$ (A.12.2); FLLM does not define it; its unpacked forms are "$b=pq+r$ where $p$ and $q$ are not in $J+K$ and $\deg_J(r)<\deg_J(b)$" (proof of Prop. 4.15) and "$b=\sum p_iq_i+r$ where … $r\in J_{\alpha+1}$" (proof of Prop. 4.4); the form "$c=ae+c'$ with $v_J(c')<v_J(c)$" is LM17's (proof of Thm. 3.3). Identical. The standard statement shape is Pitteloud's "$a\mid c\bmod(J_{v_0(c)})$ or $a\mid d\bmod(J_{v_0(d)})$" — divisibility *modulo the term's own value level* (Pit01b A.6.1; LM17 Prop. 3.2; LM24 Prop. 7.2.6). LM24 reinterprets it: "Corollary 7.2.5 translates between divisibility modulo $\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$ and divisibility in $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$" (B.2.2), and Pitteloud's "almost prime" (Pit01b A.6.2, scare quotes, once) becomes "primality in $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$". Decision: LM24 Def 7.2.2's sentence with $\equiv$; phrase "divisibility modulo $J_{\omega^\alpha}$" (LM24 A.12.2).

### III.6 Primality of $J$ and factorisation in the quotient

**Results and their history.** $J$ is prime for $G=\mathbb R$: Ber00 Cor. 9.8 (A36), a consequence of the multiplicative property ("It implies in particular that the ideal $J$ is prime", A34). For all $G$: asked by Ber00 (§13, G11), proved by Pit01a Thm 6.1 for dense $G$ — "the main result of this paper" (entry 64), with the Main Theorem 0.3 saying "for all $G$" — reported in Pit01b B.1.8 and cited by LM17 as "[Pitteloud2002]" with "for further extensions to arbitrary groups $G$, see [Biljakovic2006]" (LM17 §2.2), reproved "rather short and direct" and "where the group $\mathbf G$ is not assumed to be divisible" in LM24 Cor. 8.1.5 "([19])" (LM24 B.2.3, F.7). LM17 Prop. 2.7 "[Berarducci Thm 9.8]" [sic: Cor. 9.8] gives the two-line proof for $\mathbb R$ (§2.2). Factorisation exists in the quotient: Ber00 §13 ("The existence of the ordinal value map $v_J$ immediately ensures that there are no infinite ascending chains of principal ideals, so every element is a product of irreducible elements", B17), Pit01a Thm 6.2 for dense $G$ (entry 65), LM17 §2.7. Uniqueness in the quotient is **Berarducci's conjecture** (LM17 Conj. 1.5: "Every non-zero germ in $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))/J$ admits a unique factorisation into irreducibles", §3.3; its source is the question raised with the Kuhlmanns in Ber00 §13, B17), proved up to order-value $\omega^3$ in LM17 Thm 3.4.

**Living form and citation.** "the ideal $\mathbf J$ … is prime [2, Cor. 9.8]" (LM24 B.1.4) for $\mathbb R$; "[Pit01a, Thm. 6.1]" or "[LM24, Cor. 8.1.5]" for general $G$ (LM24 calls it "the main theorem of [19]"); "Berarducci's conjecture" with [LM17, Conj. 1.5] for uniqueness in the quotient. The bibliographic key for Pit01a is APAL 116 (2002) (header).

## Part IV. Berarducci's ordinal value and its apparatus

### IV.1 The ordinal value $v_J$

**What.** The ordinal-valued map on $K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ that sees only the tail of the support at $0$: $v_J(b)=0$ if $b\in J$; $1$ if $b\in(J+K)\setminus J$; otherwise the least order type of a series congruent to $b$ modulo $J+K$ — equivalently the order type of a "stable interval" $(-\varepsilon,0)\cap\operatorname{supp}(b)$, equivalently the last infinite term of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$.

**Identity — one object, four symbols, five names.** Ber00 Def 5.2 gives the min-over-$J+K$ definition and, in the same definition, the geometric one with stable intervals, and says they agree (A31, A32). Pit01b defines $v_0$ by the geometric clauses with $[-\varepsilon,0[$ and Remark 3 gives the Cantor-normal-form reading (A.5.1) — an *equivalent presentation*, stated as Berarducci's ("we recall some basic definitions which all appear in [1]", B.1.2). Pit01a's $v_\gamma(E)$ on sets and $v_\gamma(b):=v_\gamma(S_b)$ at any base point $\gamma\in\tilde G$ is the geometric definition with a movable base point; $v_0$ is the case $\gamma=0$ (entries 33–34). PS06's $v(a)$ is Ber00's definition restated through $\overline a$, $\widetilde a$ ("$\min\{ot(b)\mid\widetilde b=\widetilde a\}$", A.15), with Ber00's and Pitteloud's symbols acknowledged and dropped (F.1). LM17 Def 1.2 "[Def. 5.2 of Berarducci2000]" (Def 2.4 restates it without the bracket) and LM24 formula (4) and FLLM Def. (p. 4) "[Ber00, p. 558]" are Ber00's three clauses verbatim in $\equiv$-notation (LM17 §2.6, LM24 A.7.2, FLLM A.12). **All identical or equivalent; every paper after Ber00 attributes it to Ber00.** Its values are $0$ or $\omega^\alpha$ (Ber00 Rem. 5.3, A33; cited as such by LM24 B.1.1 and FLLM A.13).

Symbols: $v_J$ (Ber00 ×371; LM17 ×130; LM24; FLLM ×37), $v_0$ (Pit01b ×177, Pit01a), $v$ (PS06). Names: "ordinal-value"/"ordinal value"/"value" (Ber00 A31 — hyphenated in §1–2, 5, 9, open in §6 and §13, both the paper's), "ordinal valuation" in scare quotes and "ordinal valued map" (Pit01b A.5.1, F.2 — the quotes distance the author from "valuation"), "ordinal value" (Pit01a entry 34; PS06 A.15; FLLM A.12), "order-value" (LM17 §2.6, hyphenated, 45×), "order value" (LM24 A.7.2, "Berarducci's order value").

> **TRAP.** The bare $v$ is PS06's ordinal value and Ber00's/LM24's canonical valuation (I.7). $v_0$ in Pitteloud is the ordinal value *at $0$* and $v_\gamma$ the ordinal value *at $\gamma$*; in LM24 there is no $v_0$. Never use $v$ or $v_0$ for $v_J$.

**Decision (S4).** $v_J$, **ordinal value**, unhyphenated, cite "[Ber00, Def. 5.2]" at the definition. Argument: the symbol is unanimous in the four papers that are not Pitteloud or PS06, including both 2024 papers. The name divides the living papers — LM24 "order value", FLLM "ordinal value" — and FLLM is the later paper and returns to Berarducci's word, which Pit01a and PS06 also use; "order-value" is LM17's alone (and LM17's authors did not keep it in LM24). Fragile only in the sense that LM24's readers will have "order value" in mind; the two differ by four letters and no one will misread. Define it with the three clauses in $\equiv$-form exactly as LM24 formula (4)/FLLM do, and add LM17's Remark 2.5 in words: "$v_J(a)$ is precisely the last infinite term of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(a)$ (which is either $\omega^{\alpha_{n-1}}$ or $\omega^{\alpha_n}$, depending on whether $0\in S_a$ or $0\notin S_a$)".

**Phrasing.** "of ordinal value $\omega$" / "has ordinal value $\omega^\alpha$" (Ber00 E3 "of value $>1$", LM17 §2.6 "of order-value $\omega$" dominant, FLLM A.12 "elements of ordinal value $\omega^\alpha$"); "a series of order type $\omega$ or $\omega+1$ and order-value $\omega$" (LM17 §3.5 for Pitteloud's hypotheses); "the map $v_J$ induces …" (LM17); "$v_J(bc)$ is as big as possible" (Ber00 E3) and Pitteloud's "big"/"small" (Pit01a entry 34) are the informal register. The description "a function resembling a valuation but taking values into ordinal numbers" (LM17 §2.6) and "a specially crafted function" (LM24 B.1.1) are how the 2017–24 papers introduce it; "a new kind of valuation taking ordinal numbers as values" is Ber00's abstract (A31).

### IV.2 The local value at a point

**Identity.** Ber00 localises by translated truncation: $v_J(b^{|\gamma})$, with the hypothesis form "for every $\gamma\in X(b)$ sufficiently close to zero" (A37, A40); PS06 names it "the ordinal value of $a$ at $\gamma$" $:=v(a^{|\gamma})$ with no new symbol (A.15); LM17 and FLLM write $v_J(a^{|\gamma})$, $\deg_J(b^{|\gamma})$ (LM17 §4.2, FLLM A.13). Pit01a defines $v_\gamma(b)$ directly, for $\gamma\in\tilde G$ (entry 34), "the ordinal value of $b$ relatively to $\gamma$"; for $\gamma\in G$ this equals $v_0(b^{|\gamma})$ by translation invariance of order types — the record does not find this stated; it is immediate from entries 32–33 ($E^{|\gamma}:=-\gamma+E_{|\gamma}$, $v_\gamma(E)$ via $]\mu,\gamma]$), and Pit01a uses it silently in the proof of Theorem 5.1 ("By Lemma 3.2 we get $v_\gamma(bc)=v_0((bc)^{|\gamma})$", §5.2, p. 15). **Equivalent presentations**; Pit01a's strictly extends to $\gamma\notin G$, which is what his §7 counterexample needs ("$Z(b)\subseteq\tilde G\setminus G$", entry 66). Pit01b's "$\gamma$ is a big point of $u$" is a statement about $v_0(u^{|\gamma})$ (IV.7).

**Decision.** $v_J(b^{|\gamma})$, "the ordinal value of $b$ at $\gamma$" (PS06's phrase, the only one in the corpus); no $v_\gamma$ unless the base point leaves $\mathbb R$.

### IV.3 The multiplicative property; "(B)"; "multiplicative semi-valuation"; its failure beyond $\mathbb R$

**The result.** $v_J(bc)=v_J(b)\odot v_J(c)$ for $b,c\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ — Ber00 Thm 9.7, with Lemma 5.5 giving the ultrametric inequality and submultiplicativity (A34). Named "multiplicative property" (Ber00 ×12 standalone; §2 title), "submultiplicative property" for the inequality (×7 — the two together make the 19 raw hits of the string); "the most difficult result of this paper" (A34).

**How it is called later.** Pit01b: "Berarducci's formula, which we call (B)" (A.5.3 — the label is Pit01b's coinage, ×9; also "Lemma 2.1 d)"); Pit01a: "the multiplicative property" and "$\overset{(B)}{=}$" (entry 38); PS06: "the following multiplicative property" = Prop. 2.2 "(Lemma 5.5 and Theorem 9.7 of Berarducci [1])" (B.1 item 7); LM17: Prop. 2.6 "[Lem. 5.5 and Thm. 9.7]" with the italic parenthetical "(multiplicative property)" and "the function $v_J$ is multiplicative" (§2.7; invoked as "By the multiplicative property"); LM24: Fact 2.7.1 "([2, Lem. 5.5, Thm. 9.7])", and the *name* **multiplicative semi-valuation** for the whole package (A.8.1, B.1.1: "The key and by far most difficult result of [2] is that $v_J$ is a multiplicative semi-valuation"); FLLM: Fact 2.2 "([Ber00, Lem. 5.5, Thm. 9.7])", "The keystone of [Ber00] is that $v_J$ is a multiplicative semi-valuation" (A.12), "the multiplicativity of $v_J$" (F.1). All the same theorem. Two slips to know: FLLM's Fact 2.2 states the additive clause as "$v_J(b+c)\le v_J(b)\oplus v_J(c)$" (checked in the transcription), which is weaker than Ber00's "$\le\max$" (A34) and LM24's (Fact 2.7.1); and LM17 writes "Thm 9.8" where Cor. 9.8 is meant (§2.2).

**Failure beyond $\mathbb R$.** Ber00 asks whether it holds for general $\mathbf G$ (G11). Pit01a: no — Thm 7.1/0.4 "For many $G$, $\exists b,c$ such that $v_0(bc)<v_0(b)\odot v_0(c)$" (entry 66, F.2), e.g. $G=\mathbb R^\alpha$ lexicographic with $\alpha$ of cofinality $\omega$; yes on the subring $Re$ of real series (Thm 4.3, entry 47) and for the lower bound $(I)$ on all of $K((G^{\le0}))$ (Thm 5.1, entry 51) — both for *dense* $G$, Pit01a's standing hypothesis (VIII.1). LM24 Rem. 3.4.4 makes the parallel statement for $\deg$: "fails to be multiplicative as soon as $\mathbf G$ does not embed into $\mathbb R$" (A.9.6). Archimedean $G$ embeds in $\mathbb R$ and everything transfers (Ber00 A47 "provided $\mathbf G$ is archimedean"; Pit01b C.6).

**Decision (S10, S7).** State it as LM24 Fact 2.7.1 / FLLM Fact 2.2 do, as three clauses with the citation "[Ber00, Lem. 5.5, Thm. 9.7]" in the heading, and name it in LM24's words: "$v_J$ is a multiplicative semi-valuation [Ber00, Lem. 5.5, Thm. 9.7]", with the clauses labelled "(ultrametric inequality)", "(multiplicativity)" (V.4). "The multiplicative property" remains correct and is what one says when invoking it in a proof ("By the multiplicative property, …", LM17 §2.7). Do not use "(B)". For the failure over general $G$ cite "[Pit01a, Thm. 7.1]" (as APAL 2002); for $\deg$, "[LM24, Rem. 3.4.4]".

### IV.4 $\deg_J$ — FLLM's symbol for Berarducci's $w_J$

**Identity.** Ber00 Remark 1.1: "we can define a new map $w_J\colon K((\mathbf R^{\le 0}))/J\to OR\cup\{-\infty\}$ which behaves like a polynomial degree: $\omega^{w_J(b)}=v_J(b)$ (with $w_J(b)=-\infty$ if $v_J(b)=0$). We have $w_J(bc)=w_J(b)\oplus w_J(c)$ and $w_J(b+c)\le\max\{w_J(b),w_J(c)\}$" (A35; $w_J$ appears only there). FLLM: "let $\deg_J(b)$ denote the Cantor degree of $v_J(b)$, where by convention we set $\deg_J(b)=-\infty$ when $b\in J$, and $\omega^{-\infty}=0$. Thus we have $v_J(b)=\omega^{\deg_J(b)}$" with the same two properties (A.13; ×220, "by far the paper's dominant measure"; "we add the following notation" — no reference to Ber00's remark). **Identical** map, re-symbolled; FLLM does not say so. No other paper has it (LM24 has $\deg$, a different map, V.1). Pit01b's habit of writing every value as $\omega^{\delta+n}$ with $\delta\in\mathrm{Lim}\cup\{0\}$ (A.4.1) is the same idea done by hand.

**Decision (S6).** $\deg_J$, FLLM's symbol — the only living one — citing "[Ber00, Rem. 1.1]" for the idea and "[FLLM, §2]" for the symbol if FLLM is citable; it has no prose name in FLLM ("never '$J$-degree'", A.13) and should have none. Its relation to $\deg$: $\deg_J(b)\le\deg(b)$, "often strict, and not just for series $b\in J$" (FLLM E.7); $v_J(b)\le\omega^{\deg(b)}\le\operatorname{ot}(b)$ (LM24 A.8.4, Rem. 2.7.2). The sentence to model: "This makes the degree … quite similar to $\deg_J$, but in a sense more precise: $\deg(b)=-\infty$ only when $b=0$, whereas $\deg_J(b)=-\infty$ for all $b\in J$" (FLLM B.17; LM24 F.3 says the same of $v_J$: "$v_J$ does not satisfy condition (3)").

### IV.5 Principal value and residual value; $\deg_J^p$, $\deg_J^r$; Pitteloud's big/small split is something else

**What.** For $v_J(b)>1$ write $v_J(b)=\rho_1\cdots\rho_n$ with $\rho_1\ge\dots\ge\rho_n>1$ multiplicatively principal; the **principal value** $v_J^p(b)=\rho_n$ is the *last, smallest* factor and the **residual value** $v_J^r(b)=\rho_1\cdots\rho_{n-1}$ the rest, with $v_J^r=1$ if $n=1$ (Ber00 Def 6.4, A39; Remark 6.5: the product is the natural product; eq. (4) $v_J(b)=v_J^r(b)v_J^p(b)$). Motivation: "any series can be thought of as a series of multiplicative principal ordinal value, provided we allow the coefficients themselves to be series" (A39) — the hyper-series picture, V.8.

**Identity.** FLLM: "We define ([Ber00, Def. 6.4]): $v_J^p(b):=\omega^{\omega^{\beta_n}}$ = the principal value of $b$, $v_J^r(b):=\omega^{\omega^{\beta_1}}\cdots\omega^{\omega^{\beta_{n-1}}}$ = the residual value of $b$" (A.17) — identical, then re-symbolled "to remove the base $\omega$": $\deg_J^p(b):=\omega^{\beta_n}$, $\deg_J^r(b):=\omega^{\beta_1}+\dots+\omega^{\beta_{n-1}}$, $\deg_J(b)=\deg_J^r(b)+\deg_J^p(b)$ (A.17, F.4; the $\deg$-forms dominate: 32 and 29 against 4 and 3). Pit01a Def 4.4: $v_\gamma^r(b)$, $v_\gamma^p(b)$ "the residual/principal value of $b$ at $\gamma$", defined from the Cantor normal form with coefficients ($v^r=\omega^{\dots+\omega^{\delta_n}(m_n-1)}$, $v^p=\omega^{\omega^{\delta_n}}$) — the same two numbers at a movable base point (entry 48). PS06, LM17, LM24: absent. So: Ber00 = FLLM = Pit01a (at $\gamma=0$); equivalent presentations of one decomposition.

> **TRAP.** Pit01a §5.4 has a *different* split of the same ordinal, $v_0(X)=v^B(X)\odot v^S(X)$, with $v^B(X)=\omega^{\omega^\alpha n}$ the *leading* Cantor block with its multiplicity and $v^S(X)<\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$ the rest (entry 54; letters presumably "big"/"small", the record's inference). $v^B$ takes the top end with multiplicity; $v^p$ takes the bottom end without. For $v_0=\omega^{\omega^2 5+\omega+1}$: $v^B=\omega^{\omega^2 5}$, $v^S=\omega^{\omega+1}$ (Pit01a's example), whereas $v^p=\omega$ and $v^r=\omega^{\omega^25+\omega}$. Pit01a uses $v^B,v^S$ only in §5 (for the lower bound $(I)$) and $v^r,v^p$ in §4 and §7. Nobody later uses $v^B,v^S$.

**Decision (S6).** Ber00's names "principal value", "residual value" with FLLM's $\deg_J^p$, $\deg_J^r$ as the working symbols and $v_J^p,v_J^r$ when the base-$\omega$ form is displayed; cite "[Ber00, Def. 6.4]". Note FLLM's convention that they are defined only for $v_J(b)>1$ and that "$\deg_J(b)\neq\deg_J^p(b)$" means the Cantor normal form of $\deg_J(b)$ has more than one term (A.17).

### IV.6 The set $X(b)$ = Pitteloud's $Z(b)$ = FLLM's $\mathrm{Res}(b)$

**Identity.** Ber00 Def 6.6: "$X(b)=\{\gamma<0\mid v_J(b^{|\gamma})=v_J^r(b)\}$" for $v_J(b)>1$ (A40), "the support of this hyper-series"; Lemma 6.8: every sufficiently small final segment of $X(b)$ has order type $v_J^p(b)$ and supremum $0$ (A41). FLLM Def 2.7: "$\gamma\in\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname{supp}(b))-\{0\}$ is a **residual point** of $b$ if $\deg_J(b^{|\gamma})=\deg_J^r(b)$. The set of all these point $\mathrm{Res}(b)$ was defined in [Ber00, Def. 6.6] (where it is called $X(b)$)" (A.19, F.3) — **identical** (the domain restriction to the closed support is automatic, since $v_J(b^{|\gamma})\ge1$ forces $\gamma\in\overline{\operatorname{supp}(b)}$ by Ber00 Rem. 6.3, A38), renamed and re-symbolled with the old symbol recorded. Pit01a Def 4.4(2): "$Z(b):=\{\gamma\in\tilde G^{<0}:v_\gamma(b)=v_0^r(b)\}$", "a set $Z(b)$ of 'big points' of $b$" (entry 49), Lemma 4.5 "$v_0(Z(b))=v_0^p(b)$" — the same set in the completion; for $G=\mathbb R$ identical to $X(b)$. PS06 Def (§3): "$X(a)$ … the set of all limit points of $\mathrm{Supp}(a)$ excluding zero. In other words, $X(a)=\{x<0\mid v(a^{|x})=\omega\}$" for $\operatorname{ot}(a)\in\{\omega^2,\omega^2+1\}$ (A.18) — when moreover $\widetilde a\neq0$ (i.e. $a\notin J+K$), $v(a)=\omega^2$, $v^r(a)=\omega$, so PS06's $X(a)$ *is* Berarducci's $X(a)$, same letter, defined afresh without attribution. Edge: PS06 p. 1281 defines $X(a)$ under the order-type hypothesis alone, and for $a\in J+K$ of order type $\omega^2$ (e.g. $\sum_{n,m}t^{-1-1/n-1/m}$) PS06's $X(a)$ is still a set while $v(a)\le1$ and Berarducci's $X(a)$ is undefined; every PS06 statement that *uses* $X(a)$ assumes $\widetilde a\neq0$, so the identity holds wherever it is used (PS06 B.5 lineage table: "$V(a)$, $X(a)$ — PS06's own"). LM17: absent (its "big points" are something else, IV.7). LM24: absent.

**Decision, ⚠ FRAGILE.** FLLM's $\mathrm{Res}(b)$, "residual point(s)", with the sentence FLLM itself writes: "defined in [Ber00, Def. 6.6] (where it is called $X(b)$)". FLLM is the only living paper that has the set and it made the renaming deliberately, with a name that matches "residual value". Fragile because $X(b)$ has three papers (Ber00, PS06, and Pit01a's $Z(b)$) and FLLM one; a reader of Ber00 will need the parenthesis. Standard facts to cite with it: "[Ber00, Lem. 6.8]" for order type $v_J^p(b)$ and supremum $0$ (FLLM B.21 cites exactly this), "[Ber00, Lem. 6.9]" for lifting a lower bound along $X(b)$ — a *two-series* statement: "Let $b,c\in K((\mathbf R^{\le0}))$. If $\rho\in\mathbf H$ and $v_J(c^{|\gamma})\ge\rho$ for every $\gamma\in X(b)$ sufficiently close to zero, then $v_J(c)\ge\rho\,v_J^p(b)$" (p. 566; ordinal product). The bound is on the truncations of an arbitrary $c$ at the residual points of $b$; the one-series reading ($c=b$) is a special case, and a writer who needs only that is citing a corollary. Pit01a Lemma 3.3 is its analogue in $\tilde G$ (II.7).

### IV.7 Big points — one name, four notions

**Four things called "big point".**
1. *Pit01b* Def (p. 1211): for $v_0(u)=\omega^{\alpha+m}$, $\alpha\in\mathrm{Lim}\cup\{0\}$, $m>0$: "$\gamma\in\mathbb R^{<0}$ is a big point of $u$ if $v_0(u^{|\gamma})=\omega^{\alpha+m-1}$" (A.7.3). Since $\omega^{\alpha+m}=\omega^{\alpha+m-1}\cdot\omega$, this is $v_0(u^{|\gamma})=v_0^r(u)$ in the case $v_0^p(u)=\omega$ — i.e. *Berarducci's $X(u)$ when the principal value is $\omega$*.
2. *LM17* Def 5.4: for $v_J(a)=\omega^{\alpha+1}$, "$\gamma\in S_a$ is a big point of $a$ if $v_J(a^{|\gamma})=\omega^\alpha$" (§5.4); Remark 5.5 "the big points of a series must accumulate to $0$". The same condition as Pit01b's (with $\alpha+1$ for Pitteloud's $\alpha+m$), restricted to points of the support rather than of its closure. Identical to Pit01b's up to that restriction, unattributed.
3. *FLLM* Def 2.7: "$\gamma\in\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname{supp}(b))-\{0\}$ is a big point of $b$ if $\deg_J(b^{|\gamma})\ge\deg_J^r(b)$ and $\gamma>\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$", set $\mathrm{Big}(b)$; "More generally, $\mathrm{Big}^\alpha(b)$ … $\deg_J(b^{|\gamma})\ge\alpha$ and $\gamma>\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$" (A.18); Remark 2.8 "the big points of a series must accumulate to $0$" — LM17's sentence verbatim. Here the condition is **$\ge$, not $=$**, so $\mathrm{Big}(b)\supseteq\mathrm{Res}(b)\cap(\mathrm{crit}_J(b),0)$, with equality exactly when $\deg_J^p(b)=1$, i.e. the successor case — which is precisely the case in which Pit01b and LM17 define the term. But FLLM's definition carries a second clause, **$\gamma>\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$**, which Pit01b and LM17 do not have (Pit01b: any $\gamma\in\mathbb R^{<0}$; LM17: any $\gamma\in S_a$), and it removes points they count. So **in the successor case FLLM's $\mathrm{Big}(b)$ is the set of Pit01b/LM17 big points strictly above $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$** (and, against LM17 only, it also admits closure points outside the support): a proper subset in general, *not* the same set. Simplest case: $b=\sum_{n\ge1}t^{-1/n}$, $v_J(b)=\omega$. Pit01b/LM17 big points are all of $\{-1/n:n\ge1\}$ (at $\gamma=-1$, $b^{|-1}=1$ has value $1=\omega^0$); FLLM has $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)=\min\operatorname{supp}(b)=-1$ (FLLM p. 4: "if $b\in P_\alpha$, then $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)=\min(\operatorname{supp}(b))$"), so $\mathrm{Big}(b)=\{-1/n:n\ge2\}$. For a non-principal series the gap is infinite: $b=\sum_n t^{-2-1/(n+1)}+\sum_n t^{-1/(n+1)}$ has $v_J(b)=\omega$, Pit01b/LM17 big points $=\operatorname{supp}(b)$, $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)=-1$, FLLM $\mathrm{Big}(b)=\{-1/(n+1):n\ge1\}$. (Checked against FLLM Def. 2.5/2.7, Pit01b p. 1211 and LM17 Def. 5.4 directly; the records did not surface the $\mathrm{crit}_J$ clause's effect.)
4. *Pit01a*: "Let us say (informally) that $\gamma$ is a big point of $d$ iff $\gamma\in G^{<0}$, $\gamma$ is close to $0$, and $v_\gamma(d)$ is big (i.e. close to $v_0(d)$)" (entry 52), with the formal counterparts $Z(b)$ (= $X(b)$, IV.6 — "a set $Z(b)$ of 'big points'", entry 49) and $\mathrm{Big}(X)=\{\gamma\in\overline X: v_0(X^{|\gamma})\ge v^B(X)\}$ (entry 55) — the latter a $\ge$-condition like FLLM's but against the *big* factor $v^B$ (IV.5 trap), not the residual value. Pit01a's $\mathrm{Big}(X)$ and FLLM's $\mathrm{Big}(b)$ share a symbol and a shape and are **different sets** — even in the simplest case. The thresholds never agree: Pit01a's is the whole leading Cantor block $v^B(X)=\omega^{\omega^\alpha n}$ (in $\deg_J$ terms $\omega^\alpha n$), FLLM's is $\deg_J^r(b)$, which is $\omega^\alpha(n-1)$ when $\deg_J(b)=\omega^\alpha n$ is a single block and exceeds $\omega^\alpha n$ when lower terms follow; FLLM also excludes $0$ and everything $\le\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$. Concretely, for a single block with $n=1$, $v^B(X)=v_0(X)$ and $\mathrm{Big}(X)$ is the set of closure points whose truncation has the *full* value: for $X=\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{n\ge1}t^{-1/n})$ that is $\{0\}$ (or empty below $0$), whereas FLLM's $\mathrm{Big}(b)$ for the same series is $\{-1/n:n\ge2\}$ ($\deg_J^r(b)=0$, so the value condition is just $\gamma\in\mathrm{cl}(\operatorname{supp}(b))$). An earlier version of this map said they coincide in that case; they do not.

> **TRAP.** "Big point" means four related but distinct things: (1)=(2) on their common domain (up to LM17's "$\gamma\in S_a$", Part X.8); (3) is **not** an extension of (1)/(2) — in the successor case it is their *restriction* to $\gamma>\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$ (and otherwise replaces "$=$" by "$\ge$"), so a Pit01b/LM17 big point at or below $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$ is not a big point for FLLM; Pit01a's $Z(b)$ (which he calls big points) is $\mathrm{Res}(b)$, and Pit01a's $\mathrm{Big}(X)$ is none of the three, with a threshold that never coincides with FLLM's. Ber00 has no "big point"; it has $X(b)$ and, informally, the word "big" said of the *product*: 'The plan is to show that $v_J(bc)$ is "big" by showing that $v_J((bc)^{|\gamma})$ is "big" for "many" values of $\gamma$' (§2, p. 559; A31 list, E3).

**Decision, ⚠ FRAGILE.** If the notion is needed, FLLM's Def 2.7 (it is the living one), cited "[FLLM, Def. 2.7]", with a sentence stating the relation correctly: "in the successor case $v_J(b)=\omega^{\alpha+1}$ these are the big points of [Pit01b, §3] and [LM17, Def. 5.4] that lie strictly above $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$". Do **not** write that it "agrees with" them — it does not (item 3). Fragile on thin evidence (one arXiv paper) and because the $\mathrm{crit}_J$ clause is FLLM's alone and is exactly what makes the notions differ. The decision stands as an editorial choice; the fact under it (agreement in the successor case) was wrong and is corrected here.

### IV.8 Truncation $b_{|\gamma}$, translated truncation $b^{|\gamma}$, and LM24's $b_{\le y}$ family

**Identity.** $b_{|\gamma}:=\sum_{\beta\le\gamma}b_\beta t^\beta$ (inclusive) and $b^{|\gamma}:=t^{-\gamma}b_{|\gamma}$ — Ber00 Def 6.1 (A37), Pit01b (A.3.1–A.3.2, "we recall … which all appear in [1]"), Pit01a §3.1 (entries 31–32, with $\gamma\in\tilde G$ allowed for $b_{|\gamma}$ and $E_{|\gamma}$, $E^{|\gamma}$ for sets), PS06 ("Following Berarducci", A.13), LM17 Def 2.8 (§2.9), FLLM Def 2.4 (A.15). **Identical**, the bar rendered `|`/`\vert` before the index in every transcription (Ber00 A37 flags the rendering as unverifiable; LM17's TeX has `\vert`). LM24 Def 3.2.2 instead defines four **truncations of $b$ at $y$**: $b_{\le y},b_{<y},b_{\ge y},b_{>y}$ (A.9.1) and has *no* translated truncation; $b_{\le y}=b_{|y}$ identically. LM24 also uses "truncation" informally for the summands of a weak normal form (A.9.1).

**Names.** Ber00 names only $b_{|\gamma}$ ("the truncation of $b$ at $\gamma$") and calls the *class* of $b^{|\gamma}$ "the germ of $b$ at $\gamma$" while treating $b^{|\gamma}$ itself loosely as the germ (A37). Pitteloud names neither $b^{|\gamma}$ nor its class (Pit01b A.3.2 "No name is given"; Pit01a entry 32; "truncations" in scare quotes, entry 31). PS06 coins "**translated truncation** of $b$ at $\gamma$" for $b^{|\gamma}$ (A.13; lineage table B.5: "names … attached by PS06"). LM17 adopts both names (§2.9 "the truncation … the translated truncation … The equivalence class $a^{|\gamma}+J$ is the germ of $a$ at $\gamma$") and FLLM's Definition 2.4 is LM17's Definition 2.8 up to renaming — $b$, $b_x$, $x$ for LM17's $a$, $a_\beta$, $\beta$, the same three clauses and the same closing sentence on the germ, plus the slip "$b=\sum_\beta b_xt^x$" in its opening line (A.15). The gloss "translated truncations behave like a sort of 'generalised coefficients'" is LM17's (§2.9) and FLLM's (A.15), from Ber00's "these germs at $\gamma$ behave like generalized coefficients" (A42).

**Conventions to keep straight.** The truncation is *inclusive* at $\gamma$ and keeps the *far* (large-magnitude) side; the translation moves $\gamma$ to $0$ so that $b^{|\gamma}\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ (Ber00 A37). The truncations of "truncation closed" are strict and are a different cut (I.11). Pit01b silently allows $b^{|0}=b$ (A.5.4). FLLM uses $b_{|\gamma}$ twice and $b^{|\gamma}$ 188 times (A.15): the translated truncation is the workhorse.

**Decision (S5).** Ber00's symbols, PS06's names, FLLM's defining sentence: "the **truncation** of $b$ at $\gamma$ is $b_{|\gamma}:=\sum_{x\le\gamma}b_xt^x$, the **translated truncation** of $b$ at $\gamma$ is $b^{|\gamma}:=t^{-\gamma}b_{|\gamma}$" [Ber00, Def. 6.1]. Use LM24's $b_{\le y},b_{>y}$ only when an *upper* truncation or a split $b=b_{\le y}+b_{>y}$ is needed (LM24 A.9.1, E.4 "we are free to replace $b_j$ with $(b_j)_{\ge x_{j-1}-x_j}$"), and say once that $b_{\le y}=b_{|y}$. Do not write $b-b_{|\gamma}$ for the upper part when $b_{>\gamma}$ is available (Pit01b A.3.1 and LM17 §2.9 write the difference because they lack the symbol).

### IV.10 The Leibniz rule

**Identity.** For $v_J^p(b)\le v_J^p(c)$ and $\gamma<0$ sufficiently close to $0$: $(bc)^{|\gamma}\equiv b^{|\gamma}c+bc^{|\gamma}+\text{terms of value}<v_J^r(b)\odot v_J(c)\mod J$ — Ber00 Lemma 7.7 (A43), glossed "$b\mapsto b^{|\gamma}$ behaves like a derivation" (A43, E5) — no name. Pit01b Cor. 2.2: the $k$-th power form $(a^kb)^{|\gamma}=ka^{k-1}a^{|\gamma}b+a^kb^{|\gamma}+\varepsilon$, $v_0(\varepsilon)<\omega^{\delta+n+k-1}$, "no proof", no name, "the paper does not use the words 'derivative', 'derivation' or 'Leibniz'" (A.5.6). Pit01a Lemma 4.6 (same $k$-th power form, for Cauchy-complete $G$) and Prop. 7.2 (two-term form, $(bc)_{|\gamma}=bc_{|\gamma}+b_{|\gamma}c+$ "a term of value $v_\gamma<v_0^r(b)\odot v_0(c)$"), no name (entries 50, 67). FLLM Prop. 2.10 "([Ber00, Lem. 7.7])": "$(bc)^{|\gamma}=b^{|\gamma}c+c^{|\gamma}b+r$ where $\deg_J(r)<\deg_J^r(b)\oplus\deg_J(c)<\deg_J(bc)$" for $\deg_J^p(b)\le\deg_J^p(c)$, introduced by "one further more may obtain a kind of a Leibniz rule" (B.16). LM17 uses the mechanism (§5) without naming it; PS06 and LM24 have no such statement. **Identical** statement (Ber00 = FLLM; Pitteloud's $k$-th power version is the iterate); "Leibniz rule" is FLLM's name alone, "behaves like a derivation" Ber00's.

**Decision, ⚠ FRAGILE.** "Leibniz rule [Ber00, Lem. 7.7]" — FLLM's name, Ber00's citation; the only name on offer, in the latest paper. Keep Ber00's error-term idiom if the statement is displayed: "$+$ terms of value $<\dots$" (A43) or FLLM's explicit remainder $r$ with a bound on $\deg_J(r)$ (FLLM A.34: $r$ is the field's letter for a remainder of lower degree). The $k$-th power form is "[Pit01b, Cor. 2.2]" (and "[Pit01a, Lem. 4.6]" beyond $\mathbb R$). Ber00 §2 calls multiplying this identity by $c$ "the crucial idea" (A44), Pit01a calls it "Berarducci's trick" (entry 63) — IV.14.

### IV.11 The critical point $\mathrm{crit}(b)$; FLLM's $J$-critical point is different; LM17's "head order-value" is LM24's degree

**Critical point — identical in four papers.** Ber00 Lemma 10.1 (the set $\{v_J(b^{|x})\}$ has a maximum) and Def 10.2: "$x$ is the critical point of $b$ if $x$ is the smallest real number such that $v_J(b^{|x})=\alpha$", $\alpha$ the maximum (A46); Lemma 10.4 $v_J((bc)^{|x+y})=v_J(b^{|x})\odot v_J(c^{|y})$ for critical points $x,y$; Remark 10.3 $v_J(b^{|x})\ge v_J(b)$. PS06 restates Def 10.2 after "by Lemma 10.1 of Berarducci" without saying the *name* is Berarducci's (A.16; it is — Ber00 A46 has the bold term). LM17 Def 4.2 "from Berarducci2000", symbol $\mathrm{crit}(a)$ upright (the symbol is LM17's, §0.2), Remark 4.3: "$v_J(a^{|\mathrm{crit}(a)})$ is equal to the first term of the Cantor normal form of the order type of $a$", Lemma 4.5 "$\mathrm{crit}(bc)=\mathrm{crit}(b)+\mathrm{crit}(c)$" and multiplicativity at the critical point ("inspired by [§10]", "We proceed as in [Lemma 10.4]", §4.2); Lemma 4.4: germ-like iff $\mathrm{crit}(a)=0$. FLLM Rem. 2.6: "the notion of critical point $\mathrm{crit}(b)$, which is the minimum $\gamma$ such that $v_J(b^{|\gamma})$ is maximum possible [Ber00, Def. 10.2]" (B.12). Identical; symbol $\mathrm{crit}$ from LM17 on.

> **TRAP.** FLLM Def 2.5: "The **$J$-critical point** of $b$, denoted by $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)$, is the minimal $\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}(b)$ such that for every $\gamma<\delta<0$ we have $v_J(b^{|\delta})<v_J(b)$" (A.16), and Remark 2.6 gives $b$ with $\mathrm{crit}(b)=-2$, $\mathrm{crit}_J(b)=-1$ (F.2). **"Head order-value".** LM17 Cor. 4.7: "The function $a\mapsto v_J(a^{|\mathrm{crit}(a)})$ is multiplicative", named only in the TeX label `cor:head-order-value` and described in the introduction as "an alternative multiplicative order-value map whose value is the first term of the Cantor normal form of the order type, rather than the last infinite one" (§4.2, §10 item 3). Never call the degree a "head order-value"; say "$\omega^{\deg(b)}$ is the first term of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ [LM17, Rem. 4.3]" if the link to critical points is wanted. Use $\mathrm{crit}_J$ only with FLLM's Remark 2.6 sentence distinguishing it.

### IV.13 $\sup(b)$ and the three hypotheses

**$\sup(b)$.** Pit01b Notation: "$u^L:=\sup(S_u)\in\mathbb R^{\le0}$" (A.7.4; defined once, p. 1213, and used in one sentence of the proof of Theorem 3.3, as $b^L,c^L,d^L$); Pit01a "$b^L:=\sup(S_b)\in\tilde G$" (entry 37); LM24 Def 3.1.3 "the **supremum** of $b$ be $\sup(b):=\sup(\operatorname{supp}(b))$. **The two hypotheses — identical within each row, never across rows (S8).**

| Weaker: "$b$ is not divisible by a negative monomial" | Stronger: "the support accumulates at $0$" |
|---|---|
| $b\notin J$ (Ber00 Thm 10.5, LM17 Thm 1.1, FLLM Thm 1.1) | $b\notin J+K$ (PS06 Thms A, B) |
| $b$ not divisible by any $t^\gamma$, $\gamma<0$ (Ber00 A10, PS06 B.1, LM24 Thms E, F) | $\widetilde a\neq0$ (PS06, "Clearly, $\widetilde a\neq0$ iff $0$ is a limit point of $\mathrm{Supp}(a)$", A.12) |
| $\sup(b)=0$ for $b\neq0$ (LM24 A.12.1 "equivalently"; FLLM Def 1.2) | $v_J(b)>1$ (Ber00 A39, Def 6.4 hypothesis) |
| $v_J(b)\ge1$ (Pit01a entry 35 "$b\notin J$ iff $v_0(b)\ge1$") | "$0$ is a limit point of $\operatorname{supp}(b)$" (PS06), "$0$ an accumulation point of $\operatorname{supp}(b)$" (LM24 A.8.3, FLLM A.27), "$S_a\setminus\{0\}$ is cofinal to $0$" (Pit01b Thm 4.1, C.10), "$v_0(a)=\omega$" for type-$\omega$ series (Pit01b Thm 3.3) |

Each paper uses one column consistently and the two columns differ by exactly the constant-plus-$J$ series. Under an order-type hypothesis of the form $\omega^{\omega^\beta}$ or $\omega^\alpha$ ($\alpha\ge1$) the columns coincide, which is why PS06's Theorem 2.4 can state Ber00's Thm 10.5 with "$\widetilde a\neq0$" (PS06 B.1 item 2) and LM24 can describe Ber00's/Pit01b's criteria as "with $0$ an accumulation point of $\operatorname{supp}(b)$" (LM24 B.1.2, B.2.1) while its own Theorems E, F use the weaker column. **Decision (S8).** Write the weaker hypothesis as LM24 does — "$b$ is not divisible by $t^x$ for any $x\in\mathbb R^{<0}$" with the gloss "(equivalently, $b\notin J$; equivalently, $\sup(b)=0$)" — and the stronger as "$0$ is an accumulation point of $\operatorname{supp}(b)$ (equivalently, $b\notin J+K$; equivalently, $v_J(b)>1$)". Never let "$b\notin J$" and "$b\notin J+K$" change places within a paper.

### IV.14 Complexity inductions: the Main Lemma, Berarducci's trick, three different "complexity" maps

**Three complexities, one word.** Ber00 Defs 9.1–9.3: a *formal expression* is "an element of the free commutative monoid generated by $\{x\in K((\mathbf R^{\le0}))\mid v_J(x)>1\}$" (Def 9.1), written $b_0^{k_0}\cdots b_n^{k_n}$; the bracket $[\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_m]=\omega^{\alpha_0}\oplus\dots\oplus\omega^{\alpha_m}$ is Def 9.2; the *selected factor*, *selected exponent*, *relevant factors* and the **complexity** $\omega[\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_m]+k$ are Def 9.3 (A27, A45); the induction proves Lemma 9.6 and hence Thm 9.7. Pit01a: $Cpl\colon K((G^{\le0}))^2\times\omega\to D(OR^{<\omega})\times OR^{<\omega}\times\omega$, $(b,c,k)\mapsto(s(b),s_b(c),k)$, with the sequences $s(b)$, $s_b(c)$ of "big" factors and the non-lexicographic orders on $OR^{<\omega}$ (entries 58–60). **Three different maps**, each the paper's own (Pit01a §4 also sketches, after Lemma 4.7, a transplant of Ber00's Def 9.1–9.3 complexity to $Re$ for its Theorem 4.3; the §5 map is Pit01a's own). LM17, LM24, FLLM induct directly on the invariant: "We work by induction on $v_J(AB)$" (LM17 §3.7), "by induction on the degree" / "it is crucial that the valuation $\deg$ takes values in the ordinals, as it allows us to reason by induction on the degree" (LM24 E.3), "induction on the Cantor normal form of $\alpha$", "by induction on $\deg_J(b_1)$" (FLLM E.4).

**Berarducci's trick.** Pit01a's name for multiplying the two-term truncation identity by $b$ again (entry 63, "(see [B])"); Ber00 calls it "The crucial idea" (A44). Nobody else names it.

**Decision.** Do not import any of the complexity maps or their vocabulary ("formal expression", "selected factor", $Cpl$, $s_b(c)$); induct on $\deg$, $\deg_J$ or $v_J$ as the 2017–24 papers do, with LM24's sentence justifying it. Cite "[Ber00, Lem. 8.2]" as "Berarducci's main lemma" if needed; "Berarducci's trick" only in a remark.

## Part V. The degree and the 2024 apparatus

### V.1 The degree $\deg(b)$ and Theorem D

**What.** $\deg(b)=$ the maximum ordinal $\alpha$ with $\omega^\alpha\le\operatorname{ot}(b)$, i.e. the Cantor degree (II.5) of the order type — the *first* exponent of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$; $\deg(0):=-\infty$. **Theorem D** (LM24): $\deg(b+c)\le\max\{\deg b,\deg c\}$, $\deg(bc)=\deg(b)\oplus\deg(c)$, $\deg(b)=-\infty$ iff $b=0$ — "The degree is a multiplicative valuation" (§3 title) (LM24 A.8.2).

**Identity.** LM24 A.8.2 (unnumbered Definition in §1.5; "$\deg(b)=\deg(\operatorname{ot}(b))$", A.2.9) and FLLM Def 2.12 "([LM24, p. 5]) … the Cantor degree of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$. We also let $\deg(0):=-\infty$" with Fact 2.13 "([LM24, Thm. D])" (A.22, B.17) are **identical**, including the $-\infty$ convention (FLLM's "We also let" is a restatement, not an addition: LM24 has "We also let $\deg(0):=-\infty$", A.8.2; FLLM F.5's doubt is resolved by LM24's record). LM17's head order-value $a\mapsto v_J(a^{|\mathrm{crit}(a)})$ is $\omega^{\deg(a)}$ (IV.11; LM24 B.6.2). Ber00, Pitteloud, PS06: absent — Ber00's and Pitteloud's measure is the *last* term, $v_J$.

**History.** 2017: multiplicativity of the first term appears as a corollary of the critical-point analysis (LM17 Cor. 4.7, "Other consequences of the new order-value will be investigated in a future work", §4.2). 2024: LM24 makes it the primary valuation, names it "degree", proves Theorem D "based only on [2, Cor. 9.9]" and abandons the 2017 notation (F.4); FLLM adopts it unchanged and calls it "the degree valuation" (A.22: "In this work we also make use of the *degree valuation*"). LM24 also defines $\deg_\sigma$ for general $\mathbf G$ via the Archimedean-class projection (A.13.2) and shows $b\mapsto\deg(\operatorname{ot}(b))$ "fails to be multiplicative as soon as $\mathbf G$ does not embed into $\mathbb R$" (Rem. 3.4.4, A.9.6).

**Living form (S6).** "the **degree** of $b$, denoted by $\deg(b)$" [LM24, §1.5] (FLLM cites "[LM24, p. 5]" — that is not the published pagination: in the Adv. Math. version the Definition sits in §1.5 on p. 7, and the transcription's page markers match the article's table of contents; cite "§1.5" and do not reproduce "p. 5"); "Theorem D" is how FLLM cites the result ("[LM24, Thm. D]"); clause names "(ultrametric inequality)", "(multiplicativity)" (LM24 A.8.2, FLLM B.17). ### V.2 Principal series, weakly principal, LM17's germ-like series — and the $P_\alpha$ trap

**What.** A series $b\in K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ is **principal** if $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ is additively principal ($=\omega^\alpha$) and $\sup(b)=0$ (LM24 Def 3.3.1, A.9.2; FLLM Def 1.2, A.6: "Let $P_\alpha$ denote the set of all series $b$ such that $\operatorname{ot}(b)=\omega^\alpha$ and the supremum of the support of $b$ … is $0$. A series $b$ in $P_\alpha$ is said to be principal"). **Identical** between LM24 and FLLM (FLLM's index $\alpha$ is the Cantor degree; $P_0=K\setminus\{0\}$, $P_1$ = order type $\omega$; $0\notin P_\alpha$). For principal $b$, $\operatorname{ot}(b)=v_J(b)=\omega^{\deg(b)}$ (LM24 A.9.5 "for a principal series $b$ we have $\operatorname{ot}(b)=v_J(b)$", Prop. 7.2.1; FLLM A.23 "$\deg(b)=\deg_J(b)$" for principal $b$). **Weakly principal**: $\operatorname{ot}(b)$ additively principal, no condition on $\sup$ — LM24 only (Def 3.3.1), the hypothesis of Ber00's Cor. 9.9 as LM24 states it (Fact 3.4.1, A.9.5). Principal series "do not form a ring, because the sum of two principal series may not be principal" (LM24 Rem. 3.3.8); $P_\alpha\cdot P_\beta\subseteq P_{\alpha\oplus\beta}$ ([Ber00, Cor. 9.9]) (FLLM B.8, item (1) on p. 2). FLLM's item (2) on the same page, "all divisors of $b\in P_\alpha$ are principal ([LM17, Cor. 4.6])", and its item (3), $R_\alpha:=\{b\in P_\alpha: b\text{ reducible}\}=\bigcup_{\beta\oplus\gamma=\alpha,\ \beta,\gamma\ne0}P_\beta\cdot P_\gamma$ (A.8), are **not** to be copied — see the TRAP below.

> **TRAP — "divisors of a principal series are principal" is false, and LM17 Cor. 4.6 does not say it.** LM17 Corollary 4.6 reads: "*Let $a,b,c\in K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ be non-zero series with $a=bc$. Then $a$ is germ-like if and only if $b$ and $c$ are germ-like.*" Germ-like is strictly wider than principal (below: principal, or principal of positive degree plus a non-zero constant), and the principal version fails. Counterexample: $d=\sum_{n\ge1}t^{-1/n}\in P_1$ and $b=d(1+d)=d+d^2$. Then $\sup(b)=0$, $\deg(b)=1\oplus1=2$ (LM24 Thm D), $v_J(b)=\omega\odot\omega=\omega^2$ (Ber00 Thm 9.7) and $b$ has no constant term, so $\operatorname{ot}(b)=\omega^2$ and $b\in P_2$ (indeed $\operatorname{supp}(d)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(d^2)$ since $-1/n=-1/2n-1/2n$, and the coefficients of $d^2$ are positive integers, so $\operatorname{supp}(b)=\operatorname{supp}(d^2)$); but its divisor $1+d$ has order type $\omega+1$ and is not principal — FLLM p. 2 itself lists $1+\sum_n t^{-1/n}$ among the non-principal series. So FLLM's item (2) is a misstatement of Cor. 4.6, and its item (3) fails too: $b\in R_2$ but $b\notin P_1\cdot P_1$ (by Pit01b Thm 3.3, $d$ and $1+d$ are prime, so any factorisation of $b$ into two series of order type $\omega$ would have factors associate to $d$ and $1+d$). That is FLLM's problem; the map must not pass it on. The **correct inherited statement** is LM17's: every divisor of a germ-like series — in particular of a principal series — is germ-like, i.e. principal or principal-of-positive-degree-plus-constant [LM17, Cor. 4.6]; and on the RV side, divisors of principal RV-elements are principal: "*For all non-zero $B,C\in\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$, if $B\cdot C\in\mathrm P$ (or $\widehat{\mathrm P}$), then $B,C\in\mathrm P$ (resp. $\widehat{\mathrm P}$)*" [LM24, Cor. 6.2.2] — which is the form in which the principal version *is* true, because $\operatorname{rv}$ forgets the constant.

**History — LM17's germ-like series.** LM17 Def 4.1: "$a$ is **germ-like** if either $\operatorname{ot}(a)=v_J(a)$ or $v_J(a)>1$ and $\operatorname{ot}(a)=v_J(a)+1$" (§4.1); Lemma 4.4 "germ-like iff $\mathrm{crit}(a)=0$"; Cor. 4.6 "$a=bc$ is germ-like iff $b$ and $c$ are" — the result FLLM mis-cites as "all divisors of $b\in P_\alpha$ are principal" (TRAP above: that stronger statement is false). LM17 re-describes the whole literature in this term (§10 item 1; three sentences, p. 2 of the TeX): "The main result of [Berarducci2000] can be rephrased as saying that germ-like series of order-value $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$ are irreducible, while the main result of [Pitteloud2001] is that germ-like series of order-value $\omega$ are prime. Moreover, Pommersheim and Shahriari [Pommersheim2005] proved that germ-like series of order-value $\omega^2$ have a unique factorisation, and that some of them are irreducible." **Relation to "principal"** (worked out from the two definitions; no paper states it): the first clause, $\operatorname{ot}(a)=v_J(a)$, holds iff $a$ is principal (for $v_J(a)=\omega^\alpha\ge\omega$ the support then accumulates at $0$ and $\sup=0$; for $v_J=1$, $a\in K^\times=P_0$); the second clause holds iff $a=b+k$ with $b$ principal of degree $\ge1$ and $k\in K^\times$ (LM17 Rem. 2.5 + Lemma 4.4 proof, §4.1: "$\operatorname{ot}(a)=\omega^\alpha+1$"). So **germ-like = principal, or principal of positive degree plus a non-zero constant** (plus the series $0$, which LM17's edge case makes germ-like, §4.1, and which is neither weakly principal in LM24 — $\operatorname{ot}(0)=0$ is not of the form $\omega^\beta$, and $\sup(0)=-\infty$ — nor in any $P_\alpha$ of FLLM); "germ-like" is strictly wider than "principal", and LM24's Theorems E/F (order type $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}\mathbin{\hat+}\beta$ with $\beta<\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$; $\omega\mathbin{\hat+}k$) are wider still. LM24 does not use "germ-like" ("the paper does not import any term from [15]", C.10), and FLLM uses it only in LM17's title (C.11). The word is dead in the living literature.

> **TRAP — the symbol $P_\alpha$.** In FLLM, $P_\alpha$ is the **set of principal series** of degree $\alpha$ (Def 1.2). In LM24, $\mathrm P_\alpha:=\mathrm P\cap\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$ is the **$K$-vector space of principal RV-elements** of degree $\alpha$ together with $0$ (Def 5.2.1, A.11.1; "$\mathrm P_0=\mathbf K$", Example 5.2.2; "$\mathrm P_\alpha$ can be alternatively presented as the quotient $\mathbf J_{\omega^{\alpha+1}}/\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$", Rem. 7.2.4). The two are related by $\operatorname{rv}$: LM24's $\mathrm P_\alpha=\operatorname{rv}(\text{FLLM's }P_\alpha)\cup\{0\}$, and LM24's $\mathrm P_\alpha$ is FLLM's $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ (FLLM A.23: "$\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha=J_{\alpha+1}/J_\alpha$"). Typeface: LM24's is upright $\mathrm P$; FLLM's is italic $P$ (as transcribed). A reader of either paper will read the other's $P_\alpha$ wrongly for a page.

**Decision (S6), ⚠ FRAGILE.** "principal" with LM24's Def 3.3.1 sentence, cited [LM24, Def. 3.3.1] (FLLM's Def 1.2 is the same thing and may be co-cited). For the symbol: **follow whichever of the two systems the paper's main apparatus comes from, wholesale.** Default, the LM24 system (published, complete, built on by FLLM): upright $\mathrm P_\alpha\subseteq\mathrm{RV}$, and the *set* of principal series of degree $\alpha$ named in words ("the principal series of degree $\alpha$") or as $\{b\text{ principal}:\deg(b)=\alpha\}$. If instead the paper's main tools are FLLM's ($\deg_J$, $\mathrm{Res}$, $\mathrm{Big}$, $\mathrm{crit}_J$, $\mathrm Q$, $R_\alpha$, $A_\alpha$, $(*)_\alpha$), then FLLM's: italic $P_\alpha$ for the series and $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ for its image, with the identification "$\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ is $\mathrm P_\alpha$ in the notation of [LM24, Def. 5.2.1]" stated once. In neither case use both $P_\alpha$'s. Fragile because the two living papers disagree and the choice depends on the paper. Never "germ-like".

**Phrasing.** "$b$ principal", "for $b,c$ principal", "principal series of degree 1, with disjoint supports, thus $\mathbf K$-linearly independent by Lemma 7.1.3" (LM24 E.3), "Let $b$ principal such that $\deg_J(b)=\alpha$ and $\mathrm Q(b)$" (FLLM E.1), "non-principal elements" (FLLM A.40). Not "all divisors of $b\in P_\alpha$ are principal" (FLLM B.8) — false, see the TRAP.

### V.3 Normal form and weak normal form

**What.** LM24 Def 3.3.2 **weak normal form** $b=b_1+\dots+b_n$: each $b_i$ weakly principal, $\operatorname{ot}(b_1)\ge\dots\ge\operatorname{ot}(b_n)$, $\operatorname{supp}(b_1)<\dots<\operatorname{supp}(b_n)$ — exists uniquely for every $b\in\mathbf K((\mathbf G))_\kappa$ (Prop. 3.3.4; Rem. 3.3.3: the $\operatorname{ot}(b_i)$ are the terms of the Cantor normal form of $\operatorname{ot}(b)$) (A.9.3). Def 3.3.6 **normal form** $b=b_1t^{x_1}+\dots+b_nt^{x_n}$: $x_1\le\dots\le x_n$, each $b_i$ principal, $\operatorname{ot}(b_1)\ge\dots\ge\operatorname{ot}(b_n)$, $\operatorname{supp}(t^{x_i}b_i)<\operatorname{supp}(t^{x_{i+1}}b_{i+1})$ — unique for every $b\in\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ (Prop. 3.3.7) (A.9.4). Gloss: "any series in $\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le 0}))$ can be intuitively construed as a series with coefficients in the set of principal series, exponents in $\mathbb R$, and *finite support*" (Rem. 3.3.8).

**Identity.** FLLM Def 2.1 "([LM24, Def. 3.3.6])" restates the normal form (B.9) — **but the transcription reads "$\operatorname{ot}(b_1)\le\dots\le\operatorname{ot}(b_n)$"**. Checked: the LM24 PDF text layer has "$\operatorname{ot}(b_1)\ge\dots\ge\operatorname{ot}(b_n)$" (Def 3.3.6, p. 28), and "$\ge$" is forced (a shorter principal block immediately before a longer one would merge into a single principal series, so "$\le$" cannot give existence for, e.g., $\sum_n t^{-1-1/n}+1$). FLLM's "$\le$" is a slip in the arXiv v1 or in its transcription; the object is identical. FLLM also writes the last clause as "$x_i+\operatorname{supp}(b_i)<x_{i+1}+\operatorname{supp}(b_{i+1})$" (translate notation) where LM24 writes $\operatorname{supp}(t^{x_i}b_i)$ — same condition.

**Ancestors.** Pit01b's block decomposition in the proof of Thm 3.3: "we can write in a unique way $b=b_1'+\dots+b_r'$ such that $S_{b_1'}<\dots<S_{b_r'}\le0$, $ot(b_i')=\omega^{\beta_i}$" along the Cantor normal form of $S_b$, then modified with cut points $\theta_i$ so that "$\omega^{\beta_i}\le ot(b_i)\le\omega^{\beta_i}+1$" (A.7.5, unnamed, "parts") — the weak normal form, with the boundary points attached differently. Pit01a's $s(b)$, $s_b(c)$ sequences of big factors are a different decomposition (IV.14). Ber00's hyper-series picture (V.8) is the idea.

**Decision (S6).** LM24's two definitions verbatim, with LM24's names and "write $b=b_1t^{x_1}+\dots+b_nt^{x_n}$ in normal form" (LM24 A.9.4 phrase ×4; FLLM A.31 "be the normal form of $a$"); cite "[LM24, Def. 3.3.6, Prop. 3.3.7]". Note LM24's convention that the principal coefficient is on the left of the monomial, $b_it^{x_i}$, and that consecutive $x_i$ may coincide ("the exponent 0 appears twice in the normal form" of $1+\sum t^{-1/(n+1)}$, Rem. 3.3.10). "Cantor normal form" of an ordinal and "normal form" of a series are both in play; LM24 never says "Cantor" for the series form and never drops it for the ordinal one — keep that.

### V.4 Valuation vocabulary

**The orientation problem.** Every ordinal-valued map in this field *grows* with complexity, the reverse of Krull's convention. Ber00 says so: $v_J$ is "a non-archimedean absolute value … except that usually absolute values are defined on fields", "We can obtain a Krull valuation [Endler 72] by reversing the order", and $w_J$ "behaves like a polynomial degree" (A35, G5); he calls $v_J$ "a new kind of valuation" and speaks of "surreal valuations" (A31, F). Pitteloud puts "ordinal valuation" in scare quotes (Pit01b F.2, Pit01a entry 34). LM17: "a function resembling a valuation" (§2.6). LM24 fixes the vocabulary: "we use the word 'multiplicative' to emphasise that the ultrametric inequality uses the maximum, rather than the minimum which is more common in valuation theory" (§1.5, F.1); general definition §4: a **multiplicative semi-valuation** is $w\colon\mathbf R\to M_\infty$ with "(1) $w(b+c)\le\max\{w(b),w(c)\}$ (ultrametric inequality), (2) $w(bc)=w(b)+w(c)$ (multiplicativity)", and "$w$ is a multiplicative *valuation* when $w(b)=-\infty$ if and only if $b=0$" (A.8.1); "valued ring $(\mathbf R,w)$"; bottom value $-\infty$ with the quirk "$-\infty<-\infty$" (A.1.11); valuation ring $\mathcal O=\{w\le0\}$, maximal ideal $\mathcal M=\{w<0\}$, "residue ring" $\mathrm{RV}_0$ (A.10.5). FLLM uses "multiplicative semi-valuation" for $v_J$ and "multiplicative valuation" for $\deg$ with the same clause labels (A.12, A.22, C.9), without defining "semi-valuation".

**Identity.** LM24's vocabulary is the only defined one; FLLM's is LM24's; the earlier phrases are all apologies for the same orientation. In LM24's terms: $v$ (canonical), $\sup$, $\deg$ are multiplicative valuations, $v_J$ a multiplicative semi-valuation, $\deg_J$ (FLLM) likewise; $w_J$/$\deg_J$ and $\deg$ take values in $(\mathbf{On},\oplus)$, $v_J$ in $(\mathbf{On},\odot)$ — LM24 Table 4.4.1 lists all of them as valued rings (A.10.7).

**Decision (S7).** LM24's words exactly: "multiplicative (semi-)valuation", "(ultrametric inequality)", "(multiplicativity)", "valued ring", value $-\infty$ at $0$ (and, for $v_J$, the value $0$ on $J$ — LM24 F.3, FLLM B.17 both remark on this difference); cite "[LM24, §1.5, §4]". Do not write "absolute value", "Krull valuation", "surreal valuation", or scare-quoted "valuation"; do not use the min-convention or $+\infty$ anywhere — LM24's $\deg^-$ with $\deg^-(0)=+\infty$ (A.11.4) is proof-local and the one exception in the corpus.

### V.5 $\operatorname{rv}$, $\mathrm{RV}$, $\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$, $\mathrm P$, $\mathrm P_\alpha$, $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$, $\widehat{\mathrm P}$ — and FLLM's $\operatorname{rv}_\nu$, $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$

**LM24's construction (§4–§6), the general apparatus.** For a valued ring $(\mathbf R,w)$: $b\sim c$ iff $w(b-c)<w(b)$ (Def 4.1.1); $\mathrm{RV}:=\mathbf R_{/\sim}$, $\operatorname{rv}\colon\mathbf R\to\mathrm{RV}$ "the natural quotient map", with the product $B\cdot C:=\operatorname{rv}(bc)$ (Def 4.1.4–4.1.6, A.10.3), which "makes RV into a commutative monoid with zero" (Prop. 4.1.7); $\mathrm{RV}_m:=\{B:w(B)=m\}\cup\{\operatorname{rv}(0)\}$, an abelian group under the partial sum and an $\mathrm{RV}_0$-module (§4.2, A.10.4); $\mathrm{RV}_0$ the **residue ring** (Def 4.2.11); $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}:=\bigoplus_m\mathrm{RV}_m$ "Embedding RV into a ring" (§4.3, A.10.6); $\mathrm{RV}^*$ (A.10.7). Applied to $w=\deg$ on $\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ (§5): $\mathrm{RV}_0=\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le0})$ identified (Notation 5.1.2), $\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in\omega_1$; **principal** RV-elements, $\mathrm P$, $\mathrm P_\alpha:=\mathrm P\cap\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$ (Def 5.2.1); $\mathrm{RV}_\alpha\cong\mathrm P_\alpha\otimes_{\mathbf K}\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le0})$, "extension of scalars" (Prop. 5.3.1, A.11.2); $\widehat{\mathrm P}$ the principal elements of $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$, a subring, and $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}\cong\widehat{\mathrm P}(\mathbb R^{\le0})$ (Def 6.1.1, Prop. 6.1.2, A.11.7); "$\mathrm P$ is the RV monoid of the semi-valuation $v_J$", $\mathrm P_\alpha\cong\mathbf J_{\omega^{\alpha+1}}/\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$ (Rem. 7.2.4, A.11.1). Typography: upright RV, rv, P; RV-elements capital $B,C,D$; explicit dot for RV products (A.10.3). The source of the idea is named only as "generalising the construction of the RV group of valued fields" (A.10.1, C.2 — no citation).

**FLLM's version (Def 2.15).** "For $\nu\in\{\deg,\deg_J\}$ let $\operatorname{rv}_\nu(b)=\operatorname{rv}_\nu(c)$ iff $\nu(b-c)<\nu(b)$ or $b=c$, and let $\mathrm{RV}_\nu$ be the quotient"; $\operatorname{rv}_J,\mathrm{RV}_J$ for $\nu=\deg_J$; $\mathrm{RV}_\nu^\alpha:=\{\nu\le\alpha\}/\{\nu<\alpha\}$; "$\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha=J_{\alpha+1}/J_\alpha$"; on principal series $\operatorname{rv}_J$ and $\operatorname{rv}_{\deg}$ agree and "in this case we simply write $\operatorname{rv}(b)$"; "each $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ embeds into $\mathrm{RV}_{\deg}^\alpha$ … and coincides with the image of the principal series"; "$\mathrm{RV}_J^0\cong K$ while $\mathrm{RV}_{\deg}^0\cong K[\mathbb R^{\le}]$" (A.23). The ring structure and irreducibility in RV are used without being set up ("presupposes [LM24]'s treatment", C.14).

**Identity.** FLLM's $\sim$ is LM24's (the "or $b=c$" only repairs the $0\sim0$ case that LM24 handles with "$-\infty<-\infty$"); $\mathrm{RV}_{\deg}=\mathrm{RV}$, $\mathrm{RV}_{\deg}^\alpha=\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$, $\mathrm{RV}_J=\mathrm P$, $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha=\mathrm P_\alpha$, $\operatorname{rv}_J(b)=\operatorname{rv}(b)$ for principal $b$. **Identical objects, re-subscripted** — FLLM subscripts by the valuation because it uses two of them, LM24 needs no subscript because it applies the construction to $\deg$ only (A.10.1 "This will eventually be applied to … $w=\deg$"). FLLM credits "its properties proved in [LM24]" but presents Def 2.15 as its own (B.18).

**Decision (S6), ⚠ FRAGILE for the same reason as V.2.** LM24's unsubscripted system when the paper's valuation is $\deg$ (the default): $\operatorname{rv}$, $\mathrm{RV}$, $\mathrm{RV}_\alpha$, $\mathrm P$, $\mathrm P_\alpha$, $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$, $\widehat{\mathrm P}$, "residue ring", "principal", "extension of scalars", cite "[LM24, §4–§6]" and the numbered items (FLLM cites "[LM24, Prop. 5.3.1]", "[LM24, Lem. 7.1.1]", "[LM24, Rem. 5.4.7]" by number, B.20). When both $\deg$ and $\deg_J$ are live, FLLM's subscripts $\operatorname{rv}_J$, $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ (and then $P_\alpha$ for the series, V.2), with FLLM's sentence "$\mathrm{rv}_J$ and $\mathrm{rv}_{\deg}$ agree on the principal series" and the identification with LM24's $\mathrm P_\alpha$ stated once. Phrasing to copy: "the multiplicative, degree-preserving quotient map" (LM24 A.10.3), "$B$ divides $C$, and write $B\mid C$" (A.11.3), "$\mathrm{rv}_J(b_1),\dots,\mathrm{rv}_J(b_n)$ are linearly independent" (FLLM A.23), "$b$, $\operatorname{rv}(b)$ are irreducible" (FLLM E.2). Note FLLM says "a base" for a basis (A.23) — do not copy.

### V.6 The maximal divisor with finite support $\mathrm p(b)$

**Identity.** LM24 Prop. 5.5.1 / Notation 5.5.2: "For all $b\in\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$" (not only non-zero $b$) there is $p\in\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le0})$ with "$q\mid b$ if and only if $q\mid p$" for all $q\in\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le0})$, "unique up to multiplication by non-zero elements of $\mathbf K$" (the published text reads "$p$ in unique", p. 43); $\mathrm p(b)$ is the one whose coefficient of $t^{\sup(p)}$ is $1$; $\mathrm p(0):=0$; likewise $\mathrm p(B)$ for $B\in\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$ (Notation 5.4.5) (A.11.4). Names: "the maximal divisor with finite support", "the maximal divisor of degree 0" (A.11.4). Facts: $\mathrm p(B)=1$ for principal $B$; $\mathrm p(bc)=\mathrm p(b)\mathrm p(c)$ (Prop. 6.3.8); Theorem 6.4.1 "$b=pc_1\cdots c_n$ … $p$ is unique up to multiplication by elements of $\mathbf K$" (A.11.6). FLLM: "By [LM24, Prop. 5.5.1] … there is a unique maximal divisor $d$ such that the coefficient of $t^{\sup(d)}$ is $1$, denoted by $p(b)$" (A.25, B.19) — **identical**, same normalisation, symbol italic $p$ as transcribed against LM24's upright $\mathrm p$ (PDF-verified, LM24 header). LM24 §6.5 has a second normalisation $p_H\in1+\mathbf K(H^{<0})$ (constant term $1$) for non-complete $H$ (A.11.11) — a different normalisation for a different setting; do not mix.

**Decision (S6).** Upright $\mathrm p(b)$, "the maximal divisor with finite support", cite "[LM24, Prop. 5.5.1, Notation 5.5.2]"; FLLM's Proposition 3.2 argument — its proof opens "By Lemma 3.1 we know that $p(b)=1$ … By [LM24, Lem. 7.1.1] it is enough to prove that $\operatorname{rv}(b)/p(\operatorname{rv}(b))$ is irreducible" (p. 7 of the arXiv pagination; Lemma 3.1 itself only proves $p(b)=1$) — is the template for reducing irreducibility to the RV side (FLLM B.20; LM24 Lemma 7.2.9 "if $\mathrm{rv}(b)/\mathrm p(\mathrm{rv}(b))$ is prime (in $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$) and $\mathrm p(b)=1$, then $b$ is prime", A.12.1). Phrase: "we have already divided out the maximal divisor of degree 0" (LM24 E.2).

### V.7 The spaces $V(a)$ of PS06 and of LM17 — same symbol, different objects — and what became of them

> **TRAP.** PS06: "$V(a)=\mathrm{Span}_K\{\widetilde{a^{|\gamma}}\mid\gamma<0\}$", a subspace of $\widetilde R=R/(J+K)$, for $\operatorname{ot}(a)\in\{\omega^2,\omega^2+1\}$ — the span of *all* germs-to-the-left-of-zero of translated truncations; "our main tool"; Theorem A: "$\dim_K(V(a))>2$ implies $a$ irreducible" (PS06 A.17). LM17: "$V_\gamma(a):=\mathrm{span}_K\{a^{|\delta}+J+K:\gamma<\delta<0\}$", "$V(a):=\bigcap_{\gamma<0}V_\gamma(a)$" — the *eventual* span, the germs that "appear repeatedly … for $\delta$ approaching $0$" (LM17 §5.3); Prop. 5.3 "$a\equiv bc\mod J_{\omega^2}$, $v_J(b)=v_J(c)=\omega$ ⟹ $\dim V(a)\le2$". Both live in $K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))/(J+K)$; both are spans of germs of translated truncations; but PS06's is the span over all $\gamma$ and LM17's is the intersection of the spans over final segments. For $a$ with pairwise independent germs at its limit points, PS06's $V(a)$ is infinite-dimensional and LM17's is $0$. **Different objects, same letter.** LM17 says only "We follow a strategy similar to the one of [Pommersheim2005]" (§4.4) and does not remark on the difference.

**What became of them.** Neither survives into LM24 or FLLM. The linear algebra on germs modulo $J+K$ of order-value $\omega$ is, in 2024 terms, linear algebra in $\mathrm P_1=\mathbf J_{\omega^2}/\mathbf J_\omega$ (LM24 Rem. 7.2.4) $=\mathrm{RV}_J^1$ (FLLM): PS06's $\widetilde{a^{|\gamma}}$ for $\gamma\in X(a)$ are elements of it. FLLM replaces the dimension count by the hereditary independence relation $\mathrm Q(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ (Axiom 1 "linear independence in $\mathrm{RV}_J$", Axiom 2 the recursive clause, A.24) and the spaces $A_\alpha:=J_\alpha+\mathrm{Span}_K(R_\alpha)$ with the property $(*)_\alpha$ "$\mathrm Q(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ ⟹ $b_1,\dots,b_n$ $K$-linearly independent over $A_\alpha$" (A.28–A.29); LM24 has the lemma "principal series of degree 1, with disjoint supports, thus $\mathbf K$-linearly independent by Lemma 7.1.3" (E.3). **Decision.** Do not use $V(a)$ of either kind. If such a space is needed, define it as a subspace of $\mathrm P_\alpha$ / $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$ in 2024 language and say in a remark which of PS06's or LM17's it is.

### V.8 "Hyper-series": series whose coefficients are series

**History of one picture.** Ber00: "any series can be thought of as a series of multiplicative principal ordinal value, provided we allow the coefficients themselves to be series. … a series of ordinal value $\omega^3$ … can be also understood as a series of ordinal value $\omega$ … whose coefficients are series of ordinal value $\omega^2$" (A39); "Informally we think of $b$ as a 'hyper-series' of ordinal value $v_J^p(b)$ whose coefficients are series of ordinal value $v_J^r(b)$. The support of this hyper-series is $X(b)$. It may be challenging to find the correct algebraic framework for these hyper-series" (A40); the germs $b^{|\gamma}$ "behave like generalized coefficients" (A42). §12's iterated field $K((\mu))((H^{\le0}))$ is the rigorous version for exponent groups (A49). LM17: "we picture a series … of order-value $\omega^{\alpha+1}$ as if it were a series of order-value $\omega$ with coefficients that are themselves series of order-value $\omega^\alpha$. In other words, we describe $a$ as the sum of $\omega$ series of order-value $\omega^\alpha$" (§5.1); "translated truncations behave like a sort of 'generalised coefficients'" (§2.9; FLLM A.15 repeats it). LM24 supplies the framework Berarducci asked for, twice: for the *finite* part, the normal form "with coefficients in the set of principal series, exponents in $\mathbb R$, and finite support" (Rem. 3.3.8) and $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}\cong\widehat{\mathrm P}(\mathbb R^{\le0})$, $\mathrm{RV}_\alpha\cong\mathrm P_\alpha\otimes\mathbf K(\mathbb R^{\le0})$ (A.11.2, A.11.7); for exponent groups, $\iota_\sigma\colon\mathbf K((\mathbf G_{\preceq\sigma}))_\kappa\to\mathbf L_\sigma((H_\sigma))$ (Fact 2.4.2, VI.1). The infinite part — Berarducci's hyper-series proper, with support $X(b)$ of order type $v_J^p(b)$ — has no algebraic home in any reference: FLLM works with it by hand ($\mathrm{Res}(b)$, $\Gamma$, "a finitely generated $J_{\omega^{\alpha_1}}$-module", A.20–A.21).

**Decision.** Use "hyper-series" only in scare quotes and only citing [Ber00, §6]; say "series with coefficients in $\widehat{\mathrm P}$" / "in normal form" (LM24) for the finite version, "generalised coefficients" (LM17/FLLM, in quotes) for the heuristic. If the paper needs the infinite version as an object, that is an edge (VIII.3).

## Part VI. From real exponents to general groups

### VI.1 Archimedean classes and the reduction to $\mathbb R$ (Gonshor's theorem; Ber00 §12, Pit01b §4, LM24 §2.4/§8)

**The idea, and its three realisations.** Gonshor's observation: a series whose support meets two archimedean classes is reducible, so the interesting case is $\mathbf G=\mathbb R$ — reported by Ber00 ("In his book Gonshor sketches an argument … He first proves that in order for a series to be irreducible, the elements of its support—excluding zero—must belong to the same **archimedean class**", A51, B7) and stated by LM24 as "Fact 8.0.1 ([9, Thm. 8.6]). … If $\operatorname{supp}(b)$ intersects two distinct non-zero Archimedean classes of $\mathbf G$, then $b$ is reducible" with "it applies to any $\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa$ without modifications" (B.3.2, F.6). FLLM: "A crucial part of the argument, first suggested by Gonshor [Gon86], is the reduction to the ring $K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$" (B.4).

1. *Ber00 §12* (Mourgues's suggestion, B8): for an archimedean subgroup $Q$ with a chosen $1\in Q$, $Fin$ = the smallest convex subgroup containing $1$, $\mu$ = the largest convex subgroup not containing $1$, $Fin=H\oplus\mu$ with $H\simeq Fin/\mu$ archimedean and the order lexicographic; the canonical identification $K((Fin^{\le0}))\subset K((\mu))((H^{\le0}))=F((H^{\le0}))$ with $F:=K((\mu))$ a field; "the units of $F((H^{\le0}))$ are the elements of $F=K((\mu))$, while those of $K((Fin^{\le0}))$ are the elements of $K$" (A49). Theorem 12.1: $a+1$ irreducible in $K((\mathbf G^{\le0}))$ for $a\in K((Q^{<0}))$ of order type $\omega$ or $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$ not divisible by a monomial of $Q^{<0}$; "$\mathbf G$ can be allowed to be a proper class. So Conway's series … is irreducible even in the ring of omnific integers" (A49).
2. *Pit01b §4*: $G_0$ a **maximal proper convex subgroup**, $G=H\oplus G_0$ with $H$ archimedean and $Q\subseteq H$; "a canonical ordered fields isomorphism $i\colon K((G))\to K((G_0))((H))$" with $i(K((G^{\le0})))\subseteq K((G_0))((H^{\le0}))$; Theorem 4.2: $a+1$ prime in $K((G^{\le0}))$ (A.8.5), "The proof of the next theorem is exactly the same as in [1], so we only sketch it" (B.1.7). This is Ber00's construction in the special case where $\mu=G_0$ is the *top* proper convex subgroup (so $Fin=G$). Theorem 4.1 handles archimedean $G$ directly by embedding in $\mathbb R$ (A.8.4).
3. *LM24 §2.4, §8*: the general apparatus. $x\preceq y$ iff $|x|\le n|y|$, $\asymp$, $\prec$; the **Archimedean class** $[x]$; $\mathbf G_{/\asymp}$ (A.4.1); for a class $\sigma$: $\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}$, $\mathbf G_{\preceq\sigma}$, the field $\mathbf L_\sigma:=\mathbf K((\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}))_\kappa$, the ring $\mathbf S_\sigma:=\mathbf L_\sigma\cap(\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa)$ (Notation 2.4.1); Fact 2.4.2: a complement $H_\sigma$ of $\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}$ in $\mathbf G_{\preceq\sigma}$, Archimedean, and "the map $\iota_\sigma\colon\mathbf K((\mathbf G_{\preceq\sigma}))_\kappa\to\mathbf L_\sigma((H_\sigma))$ … is a field isomorphism" restricting to a ring isomorphism onto $\mathbf S_\sigma+\mathbf L_\sigma((H_\sigma^{<0}))$; "The above are just standard considerations in the theory of divisible ordered abelian groups and Hahn series" (A.4.3–A.4.4, citing "[2, §12], [4] for considerations along these lines", B.1.6). §8 adds the projections $T_\sigma$, $\tau_\sigma$ (ring homomorphisms, Def 8.1.1), $\deg_\sigma$, $\sup_\sigma$ (Cor. 8.1.4), $\rho_\sigma$ (Def 8.2.4), **reduced** series (Def 8.2.6: $\operatorname{supp}(b)\cap\operatorname{supp}(b-1)$ within one Archimedean class), **pseudo-support** $\overline{\operatorname{supp}}(b)$, **pseudo-polynomial**, **pseudo-monomial**, **pseudo-irreducible**, **almost irreducible** (first defined in the running text of §6.5, p. 52: "for every divisor $c\in\mathbf K((H^{\le0}))$ of $b$, if $c$ is not a monomial, then $b/c$ is"; Def 8.3.1 re-defines it by reference), $\mathrm R(b)$ and the infinite product $\dot\prod$ (A.13.1–A.13.11).

**Identity.** One construction: Ber00's $\mu$, $Fin$, $H$, $F$ are LM24's $\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}$, $\mathbf G_{\preceq\sigma}$, $H_\sigma$, $\mathbf L_\sigma$ for $\sigma=[1]$; Pit01b's $G_0$, $H$, $i$ are LM24's $\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}$, $H_\sigma$, $\iota_\sigma$ for $\sigma$ the top class. Ber00 and Pit01b have no notation for Archimedean classes as objects; LM24 does. **Equivalent presentations**, LM24's being the general one and the only one with names.

**Spelling.** "archimedean" lower-case (Ber00 A51: ×12 as the bare word, plus ×5 "non-archimedean" and ×2 "nonarchimedean" in bibliography titles — 19 raw hits, 0 capitals; Pitteloud ×7); "Archimedean" capital (LM24 throughout: ×35, 0 lower-case). **Decision.** LM24 wholesale: "Archimedean class", $[x]$, $\preceq,\asymp,\prec$ (LM24's $\prec$ is the *magnitude* order: $x\prec y$ means $x$ is infinitesimal relative to $y$, A.4.1 — do not confuse with a valuation-theoretic $\prec$), $\mathbf G_{\prec\sigma}$, $H_\sigma$, $\mathbf L_\sigma$, $\iota_\sigma$; cite "[LM24, §2.4]" and add "see also [Ber00, §12]" as LM24 does. "maximal proper convex subgroup" is Pit01b's phrase (A.9 table; LM24 B.2.1 drops "proper"). For the irreducibility/primality of $a+1$ over general $\mathbf G$: "[Ber00, Thm. 12.1]" and "[Pit01b, Thm. 4.2]"; for $\mathbf{Oz}$: "[Ber00, Rem. 12.2]".

### VI.2 Completions: Pitteloud's $\tilde G$, $G'$ and LM24's $\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa$

**Pit01a §2.** $\tilde G:=\{$initial segments of $G$ without largest element$\}$, ordered by inclusion, with $-\infty=\varnothing$, $\infty=G$, $I+J:=\{\gamma+\gamma'\}$; "the completion"/"order-completion" (entry 19); complete, addition left-continuous but not right-continuous (entry 22); defined for any dense ordered semi-group with left-continuous addition (entry 17). $G'$ the **Cauchy-completion**, $=$ the regular points $\tilde G\setminus(\tilde G^i\cup\{\pm\infty\})$, an ordered group; $\tilde G^i$ the irregular points where $\mu+x=\mu$ for some $x\ne0$ (entries 23–24). Closures $\overline X$ in $G'$, $\overline{\overline X}$ in $\tilde G$ (entry 39). $b^L:=\sup S_b\in\tilde G$ (entry 37). Examples: $\tilde{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R\cup\{\pm\infty\}$; $\widetilde{\mathbb R^2}$ (entry 21). The results are "known or easy … For details, see [Pi1]" (the thesis, B13).

**LM24 §8.5.** $L(A)$, $U(A)$, $A^\#:=L(U(A))$; the **restricted Dedekind–MacNeille completion** $\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa:=\{A^\#:A\subseteq\mathbf G\}$, ordered by inclusion, with $\eta\oplus\zeta:=(\eta+\zeta)^\#$ — "we use $\oplus$ for the sum in the completion of $\mathbf G$ to avoid ambiguities; also, we tweak the definition of completion so that it works when $\mathbf G$ is a proper class." and, as a separate paragraph, "Note that here *we do not assume that $\mathbf G$ is divisible*." (§8.5 preamble before Def 8.5.1, A.14.1, citing Birkhoff [3, §XIII.13], F.9); then a quotient $\tilde{\mathbf G}_\kappa^{\le0}:=\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa^{\le0}/{\sim}$ with $\eta\sim\zeta$ iff "for every $n$ and $n\eta\le x,y\le n\zeta$ … we have $x-y\prec y$" (the relation $\sim$ is Def 8.5.3; the quotient and the class-valued $\sup$ are Def 8.5.5), and $\sup(b):=$ the $\sim$-class of $\operatorname{supp}(b)^\#$, "a multiplicative valuation" (Prop. 8.5.6, A.14.3). "This will not be used in the rest of the paper, so the reader is free to skip" (E.5).

**Identity — partly.** $\tilde G$ and $\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa$ are both the Dedekind–MacNeille completion of $G$ as a linear order: Pitteloud's cuts are the *open* initial segments (no largest element, $I_\gamma=\{\gamma'<\gamma\}$), LM24's are the *closed* lower sets $L(A)$ ($x^\#=(-\infty,x]$); for a dense $G$ these are in order-preserving bijection, so the ordered sets are **equivalent presentations** (up to LM24's $\kappa$-restriction). Pitteloud's $\tilde G$ contains the two end points by definition ($-\infty=\varnothing$, $\infty=G$, entry 19). That LM24's $\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa$ does too is **the map's inference, not LM24's statement**: LM24 never discusses the end points; under the first presentation $\{A^\#:A\subseteq\mathbf G\}$ one gets $\varnothing^\#=L(\mathbf G)=\varnothing$ and $\mathbf G^\#=L(\varnothing)=\mathbf G$, while under the second, "$\{L(A):A\subseteq\mathbf G,\ A\text{ set},|A|<\kappa\}$", the bottom element requires a set $A$ coinitial in $\mathbf G$ of size $<\kappa$; the only related text is the proof of Prop. 8.5.6, "$\sup(0)=[\varnothing^\#]=:-\infty$, the minimum element" (p. 78). Unverified as a statement of LM24. The additions are defined differently (Minkowski sum of open segments versus $(\eta+\zeta)^\#$); whether they correspond under the bijection is not settled by the records — the natural guess is yes for dense groups, but neither paper cites the other and LM24 cites Birkhoff. Pitteloud's $b^L\in\tilde G$ and LM24's $\sup(b)$ in the *quotient* $\tilde{\mathbf G}_\kappa^{\le0}$ are **different** (LM24's is coarser by design, so that $\sup$ is multiplicative). Pitteloud's Cauchy-completion $G'$ has no counterpart in LM24.

**Decision, ⚠ FRAGILE.** If a completion is needed and $\mathbb R$-valued $\sup$ is not enough: LM24's $\overline{\mathbf G}_\kappa$ and its notation (the only one in a living paper; LM24 §8.5), with a footnote that it is the order-completion $\tilde G$ of [Pit01a, §2] up to the choice of open versus closed cuts. Fragile because the correspondence of the additions is unverified here (Part X) and because LM24 itself marks the section optional. Pitteloud's $G'$, $\tilde G^i$, "left-continuous" belong to the one-paper edge (VIII.1).

### VI.3 Pitteloud's real series $Re$

**Only Pit01a.** "$Re:=\{b\in K((G^{\le0})):\overline{\overline{S_b}}\subseteq G'\}$ … [Observe that if $G=(\mathbb R,+)$, then $Re=K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$]", "the subring $Re\subseteq K((G^{\le0}))$ of 'real' series" (entry 47 — the quotation marks occur once, in the introduction, p. 2; the §4 title and Def 4.1 say "real series" unquoted); Theorem 4.3: the multiplicative property holds on $Re$ for dense $G$; Cor. 4.8: $J':=J\cap Re$ prime in $Re$, factorisations exist in $Re/J'$ (entry 35; the preprint's Cor. 4.8(ii) prints "the quotient ring $Re/J$" where $Re/J'$ is meant — corrected here, silently in the earlier version of this map). $Z(b)\subseteq(G')^{<0}$ for $b\in Re$ (entry 49). The name is not used by any later paper. Its role — the subring on which the $\mathbb R$-theory survives for a non-archimedean $G$ — is played in LM24 by the reduction along Archimedean classes (VI.1) and by $\mathbf L_\sigma((H_\sigma^{\le0}))$ with $H_\sigma$ possibly non-complete (§6.5, A.11.10). **Decision.** Do not use $Re$; if the condition "the closure of the support lies in the Cauchy completion" is needed, state it in words and cite "[Pit01a, Def. 4.1]".

### VI.4 Transfer of irreducibility, primality and primality-like properties

**What is transferred and by whom.** Irreducibility of $a+1$ from $K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ to $K((\mathbf G^{\le0}))$, $\mathbf G$ possibly a class: Ber00 Thm 12.1, Rem. 12.2 (A49). Primality of $a+1$ when $G$ has a maximal proper convex subgroup: Pit01b Thm 4.2 (A.8.5). Primes remain prime in $K((G^{\le0}))$ for $G\supseteq\mathbb R$: Biljakovic–Kochetov–Kuhlmann [4, Thm. 4.12] as reported by LM24 (B.5.3) — this, on top of Pit01b Thm 3.3, is what makes Conway's series prime in $\mathbf{Oz}$ (LM24 §1.3; I.10). Primality *and primality-like* properties (primal) for $\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa$: LM24 Prop. 8.2.9 ("a suitable primality transfer result (Proposition 8.2.9, inspired by [4, Cor. 4.3])", §1.4 p. 5), Lemma 9.2.1 "an abstract transfer lemma", Prop. 9.2.2 ("$b$ is primal in $\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa$ if and only if $\iota_\sigma(b)$ is primal in $\mathbf L((H_\sigma^{\le0}))$", described as "a generalisation of [4, Cor. 4.3]" on p. 6, "generalising [4, Cor. 4.3]" on p. 79, and introduced on p. 82 as "the following generalisation of Proposition 8.2.9 and of [4, Cor. 4.3]" — the phrase "inspired by" belongs to Prop. 8.2.9, not to 9.2.2), Theorem 9.0.1 with conditions (A1)$_\sigma$–(A3) (A.15.1–A.15.3). Reduction to the real case for factorisation: LM24 Thm 8.4.8 / Theorem C via reduced factors and $\dot\prod$ (A.13.11).

**Living vocabulary.** LM24's verbs: "transfer", "lift" ("lifts to a primal or prime series", "It is now easy to lift the above result to primality in $\mathbf K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$"), "reduce to the case of series with real exponents", "Translating to real powers" (§8.1 heading), "primal elements in $\mathbf L((H_\sigma^{\le0}))$ yield primal elements of $\mathbf Z+\mathbf K((\mathbf G^{<0}))_\kappa$ via the map $\iota_\sigma$" (E.4). Ber00's: "reduce to the special case $\mathbf G=\mathbb R$ at the expense of enlarging the field of coefficients $K$" (E8) — still the clearest one-line description of what $\iota_\sigma$ does. Pit01b's "remains prime"/Ber00's "remains irreducible" (Ber00 A9: "Any irreducible element of $K[\mathbf R^{\le0}]$ remains irreducible in $K((\mathbf R^{\le0}))$") for the direction finite-support → all series. **Decision.** LM24's apparatus and verbs; cite "[LM24, Prop. 9.2.2]" for primal transfer, "[Ber00, §12]" and "[Pit01b, §4]" for the classical irreducible/prime cases, [4] as LM24 cites it for the $\mathbb R\subseteq G$ case.

## Part VII. Results the field refers to by name, and how it cites them

### VII.1 Citation style (S10)

The 2017–24 papers cite a result by its number in the original, inside the heading of the restated statement: `\begin{thm}[{\cite[Thm.\ 10.5]{Berarducci2000}}]` (LM17 §8), "**Fact 2.7.1** ([2, Lem. 5.5, Thm. 9.7])" (LM24 B.1.1), "**Proposition 2.9** ([Ber00, Lem. 7.5(2)])", "**Definition 2.12** ([LM24, p. 5])" (FLLM B). Abbreviations: Thm., Prop., Lem., Cor., Def., Rem., p., § (LM17 §8; FLLM B). Black-box imports are headed "Fact" (LM24 A.9.5, FLLM E.2: "Imported results are labelled 'Fact' when quoted as black boxes"); restatements in the paper's own notation are headed Theorem/Proposition/Definition with the source in the bracket. PS06's "**Proposition 2.1** (Lemma 7.5 of Berarducci [1])" (A.19) is the same convention in words. Pit01b's "(see [1])" and Pit01a's "[B]" keys are older style. Berarducci's own "Fact" is reserved for results quoted from the literature (Ber00 A56) — the same usage. **Decision.** Headings with the pinpoint citation in brackets; "Fact" for imports; the result's *name* in an italic parenthetical inside the statement when the field has one ("(multiplicative property)", LM17; "(convolution formula)", LM17/FLLM; "(ultrametric inequality)", "(multiplicativity)", LM24/FLLM).

### VIII.3 Where the shared universe stops — what a new paper must define, and in what style

**What the references do not define.** Reading the seven records for what they reach toward but leave undefined, the shared universe stops at these boundaries:

1. *An algebraic home for the infinite "hyper-series".* Ber00 asks for it (A40); LM24 gives one for the finite-support layer ($\widehat{\mathrm P}(\mathbb R^{\le0})$, normal form) and none for series whose "hyper-support" $X(b)$ is infinite; FLLM handles that layer by hand. Any object that packages $b$ as a series over $\mathrm{Res}(b)$ with coefficients in $\mathrm P_{\deg^r}$ is new.
2. *Filtration and graded-ring vocabulary around $J_{\omega^\alpha}$.* The references have the spaces (III.4), know when they are rings (LM17 §3.6: "closed under multiplication iff $\alpha=\omega^\beta$"; FLLM A.21: "for $\alpha$ additively principal also a ring"), use modules over them (FLLM: "a finitely generated $J_{\omega^{\alpha_1}}$-module", "$J_\alpha$-linear combinations"), and identify the associated quotients ($\mathrm P_\alpha\cong\mathbf J_{\omega^{\alpha+1}}/\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$, LM24 Rem 7.2.4; $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha=J_{\alpha+1}/J_\alpha$, FLLM) — but no paper says "filtration", "associated graded", "$\mathfrak a$-adic", or treats $(J_{\omega^\alpha})_\alpha$ as a structure. A paper that does must define the structure and then say, in a remark, that its degree-$\alpha$ piece is LM24's $\mathrm P_\alpha$ and that $\widehat{\mathrm P}$ is its associated graded ring (LM24 Rem 7.2.4 gives the sentence: "$\mathrm P$ is the RV monoid of the semi-valuation $v_J$").
3. *Derivation language.* Ber00's "$b\mapsto b^{|\gamma}$ behaves like a derivation" (A43) and FLLM's "a kind of a Leibniz rule" (B.16) are similes; no reference defines a derivation, a differential operator, or a higher Leibniz rule on $K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ or on $\widehat{\mathrm P}$. Anything of that kind is new and must be defined from scratch; the Leibniz rule of IV.10 is the only inherited statement to attach it to.
4. *Primality-like properties of individual series beyond "primal".* LM24 §9 has "primal in", the transfer lemma, and the conditions (A1)–(A3); Pit01b has the informal "almost prime"; LM24 has "almost irreducible" and "pseudo-irreducible" for the $\mathbf G$-setting. Intermediate notions (prime modulo a filtration level, prime in $\widehat{\mathrm P}$ versus in $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$, primal at a level) exist only as LM24's reinterpretation of Pitteloud's Prop. 3.2 ("a statement about primality in $\widehat{\mathrm{RV}}$", B.2.2). They must be defined; LM24 §7.2 is the style.
5. *The refinement/pre-Schreier question for $K((\mathbb R^{\le0}))$ itself.* Open in every paper (Ber00 §13; LM17 Conj. 1.4; LM24 Cor. 6.4.2 reduces it to "every irreducible series with infinite support is prime" and §7 says "it is still an open question whether all irreducible series … are prime"; LM24's Question on $t^HK((H^{\le0}))$). Any partial answer needs LM24's vocabulary (I.9) and nothing else exists.
6. *Uniqueness of factorisation in the quotient* beyond $\omega^3$ (Berarducci's conjecture, LM17 Thm 3.4) and *existence of irreducibles of every order type* (LM24 "It is reasonable to expect", A.12.3; FLLM "$3\omega+2$ is now the smallest $\alpha$ for which we do not know", E.5).
7. *Completions beyond $\mathbb R$* (VI.2) and *algorithms* (LM24 A.11.5: Remark 5.4.7 says the proof of Prop. 5.4.3 "is also an algorithm to compute the maximal divisor" of finite support; the sentence "we do not know an algorithm to determine when a series with infinite support is irreducible, and the task seems highly not trivial" is in §5.6, the paragraph after Proposition 5.6.1, p. 45 — cite "[LM24, §5.6]" for it, not the Remark).
8. *Higher-level Leibniz/convolution identities* (Ber00's Remark 7.6 for several factors; Pit01b's and Pit01a's $k$-th power forms) stop at first order in the truncation; nothing beyond.

**In what style.** The records show one house style for introducing a notion that the readers will accept as an extension rather than a new dialect:

- *Extend by qualifying the old word, keep the old symbol and subscript it.* FLLM: "$J$-critical point", $\mathrm{crit}_J$, next to Berarducci's $\mathrm{crit}$ with "One should not confuse this with" and a worked example (A.16, F.2); $\deg_J$, $\operatorname{rv}_J$, $\mathrm{RV}_J^\alpha$. LM24: "weakly principal", "almost irreducible", "pseudo-polynomial", "restricted Hahn field", $\deg_\sigma$, $\sup_\sigma$, $\mathbf J_{\omega^\alpha}$. Never a fresh letter for an old thing.
- *Define generally, then specialise.* LM24 §4 builds RV for an arbitrary valued ring $(\mathbf R,w)$ and only then sets $w=\deg$ (A.10.1); §8.5 the same for completions. A new structure should be stated for a general $(\mathbf R,w)$ or for a general filtration and then applied.
- *Definition sentences.* "We say that $b$ is **X** if …", "Call **X** the …", "let $\mathrm X(b)$ denote …", "Given $b$, let the **X** of $b$, denoted by $\mathrm X(b)$, be …" (LM24 E.1; FLLM E.6); bold at the defining occurrence, italic at a preview in the introduction (LM24 E.1); numbered Definition environments with the source in brackets when there is one; "$:=$" for definitions (FLLM A.8).
- *Identify, and say so.* "With a slight abuse of notation, we shall identify …" (LM24 E.4; LM17 §2.6); "For clarity, note for instance that $\mathbf J_{\omega^0}=\mathbf J_1=\mathbf J$, $\mathbf J_{\omega^1}=\mathbf J_\omega=\mathbf J+\mathbf K$" (LM24 Def 7.2.2, p. 57; A.12.2) — every new indexed family gets its first two members spelled out (and note the bare indices $\mathbf J_1$, $\mathbf J_\omega$ in that sentence: they are $v_J$-bounds, the III.4 trap in LM24's own hand).
- *Re-symbolling is announced with the old symbol.* "(Berarducci [1] uses $v_J(a)$ and Pitteloud [6] uses $v_0(a)$)" (PS06 A.15); "(where it is called $X(b)$)" (FLLM A.19); "For the sake of readability, we introduce the following notation to remove the base $\omega$" (FLLM A.17); "with a considerably different notation" (LM24 F.4).
- *Attribution at the point of use*, in the heading, by number (VII.1); black boxes headed "Fact".
- *Corrections are quoted and exemplified.* FLLM's Example 2.3 against "few cancellation" (F.1); LM24's "We thank Pietro Freni for noticing an error in an earlier version" (F.13).
- *The informal register is fenced with quotation marks*: Berarducci's "hyper-series", "big", "roughly" (E10); Pitteloud's "real" series, "ordinal valuation", "truncations" (E10); LM17's `tail'; LM24's "quirk". A heuristic word that is not defined goes in quotes, once.
- *Open problems are stated as such*: "It is reasonable to expect that …", "it is still an open question whether …", "We conjecture that …, and more precisely, we expect the following to hold" (LM24 E.5; FLLM E.2).
